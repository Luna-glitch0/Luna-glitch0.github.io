1인칭 일본 드라마
제작 가이드

카메라가 인물의 눈이 되는 실사 드라마를 만드는 법. «자연스러움» 은 취향이 아니라 통제할 수 있는 항목들의 합입니다. 그 항목을 열두 개로 나눠 정리했습니다.

Part Ⅰ

시작하기

설정을 맞추고, 워크플로를 고르고, 고정 블록을 붙입니다. 여기까지는 매번 같습니다.

설정

항목기준값
모델seedance_2_5
화면비9:16
해상도480p
비트레이트High
오디오On
작업 단위 길이15초

15초는 지금 쓰는 단위일 뿐 규격이 아닙니다. 이전 채택본에는 19초짜리도 있습니다.

비트레이트를 올려도 연기는 나아지지 않습니다.

High 는 압축으로 뭉개지는 것만 막아줍니다. 인물이 같은 사람으로 보이는지, 연기가 자연스러운지는 프롬프트와 레퍼런스가 정합니다. 화질이 아쉬울 때 비트레이트를 올리는 것은 맞지만, 연기가 어색할 때 올리는 것은 헛수고입니다.

워크플로 둘

스틸에서 시작
Start-frame 생성

구도를 스틸로 먼저 확정하고 그 상태에서 움직입니다. 왜곡이 가장 적었던 방식입니다.

이어 붙이기
Forward 확장

채택한 클립의 실제 마지막 프레임에서 잇습니다. 입장 씬이 이 방식으로 나왔습니다.

둘 다 연속성을 보장하지는 않습니다. 이어 붙였다고 이어진 것이 아니라, 움직임 · 대사 · 인물 동일성 · 이음매를 눈으로 확인한 뒤에야 이어진 것입니다.

시작 프레임을 확실히 물리는 한 줄

@Image 1 IS THE FIRST FRAME. The shot begins exactly on it - same framing,
same objects in the same places, same light. Nothing is re-staged or added at the start.
Start image 프롬프트 — 실제 발췌 
Create a 15-second vertical 9:16 photorealistic Japanese live-action drama with synchronized Japanese dialogue and realistic location sound.

Continue directly from the supplied starting frame.

Match the starting frame's camera position, framing, Hinata's facial identity, hairstyle, clothing, door opening angle, hand placement, lighting, and entrance layout.
Extend Video 프롬프트 — 실제 발췌 
Extend the uploaded video FORWARD with a 15-second continuation in vertical 9:16.

Begin directly from the source video's actual final frame and continue its ongoing movement naturally. Preserve Hinata's position, pose, gaze direction, door angle, camera position, framing, lighting, and visible environment.

Maintain one uninterrupted first-person shot from the visitor's eye level. Do not restart the scene, replay preceding actions, or repeat dialogue from the source video.

All timestamps below refer only to the newly added 15-second continuation.

Forward 확장에서는 소스 영상만 올리고 Start · End 이미지는 넣지 않습니다. End 레퍼런스를 넣어도 끝 구간이 원본과 똑같이 남지는 않습니다.

고정 블록

씬이 바뀌어도 그대로 쓰는 부분입니다. 동작 · 대사 · 타이밍은 여기에 섞지 않고 따로 붙입니다. 섞으면 다음 씬에서 어디를 고쳐야 하는지 알 수 없게 됩니다.

고정 1인칭 블록 전문 
ONE uninterrupted take, entirely from the first-person viewpoint of a new resident arriving at a Japanese share house. The camera IS the visitor's eyes. All movement happens in real time within one continuous physical space.

CAMERA AND FRAMING
Strict eye-level first-person viewpoint.
Maintain a fixed focal length throughout.

Door frames, roof edges, wall corners, and tile lines remain straight across the image.
Natural perspective convergence during head tilts is acceptable, but architectural lines must not bow or curve.

Frame only what is naturally visible from the visitor's position.
Allow the roof, ground, suitcase, and Hinata's lower body to extend outside the frame.
Do not widen the lens to fit everything into one image.

When stopped, retain very slight natural postural sway.
Camera movement must not bend, stretch, or warp the environment.
Never show the visitor's face or full body.

Camera movement is visual only and must not produce audible breathing.

CHARACTER PERFORMANCE
Whenever she addresses the visitor, she looks directly into the camera lens.
Play this with small, believable reactions rather than broad comedy.
Preserve her normal facial proportions.
No exaggerated wide eyes, stretched mouth, or theatrical shock.

GLOBAL INTERACTIVE AUDIO RULE
The visitor NEVER produces audible dialogue, breathing, sighs, gasps, humming, laughter, or other vocal sounds.
Natural camera movement is visual only and must not be accompanied by breath noise.
Footsteps, suitcase wheels, and physical interaction sounds are allowed.

Whenever the script reaches a visitor response, replace the spoken response with approximately FIVE SECONDS of the other character looking into the lens and naturally waiting or listening.
No audible visitor answer, subtitles, response text, or countdown.

Use occasional natural blinking and tiny relaxed posture adjustments.
Her mouth remains relaxed and still.
She does not speak again or react as though an answer has already been confirmed.

CONTINUOUS ACTION
These timestamps are successive beats within ONE uninterrupted take, not separate shots.
Preserve all actions in order.
Use normal-speed movement with connected transitions between actions.

FINAL CONSTRAINTS
No cuts, hidden cuts, transitions, montage, reverse angles, inserts, time jumps, slow motion, or speed ramps.
No subtitles, captions, speech bubbles, countdowns, logos, or text overlays.
이 블록에서 가장 자주 틀리는 것

«입을 편히 다물고 있다», «대답을 들은 것처럼 굴지 않는다» 는 5초 응답 구간에만 적용됩니다. 대사 중에는 아닙니다.

이걸 씬 전체에 적용하면 배우가 말을 하다 말고 굳습니다. «알아들었다» 는 반응이 필요하면 5초가 끝난 뒤에 따로 적습니다. 채택된 입장 씬은 그 자리에 작은 끄덕임과 「はい！あ、どうぞ。」 를 넣었습니다.

Part Ⅱ

연기를 쓰는 법

감정 이름을 적는 것으로는 연기가 정해지지 않습니다. «놀랐다» 만 주면 놀람의 크기를 모델이 정하고, 대개 크게 정합니다.

① 이유를 주고, 보이는 반응을 지정한다

무엇을 예상했고, 무엇이 달라졌고, 무엇을 감추려 하는지를 씁니다.

감정 이름 하나는 연기의 대부분을 미정으로 남깁니다. 미정인 부분은 모델이 채우고, 그 결과가 «과장» 으로 보이는 겁니다.

그래서 순서를 줍니다. 기대 → 계기 → 작은 신체 반응 → 사회적 수습. 한 순간에 강한 감정을 여러 개 쌓지 않습니다. 가벼운 민망함은 머뭇거리는 시선과 절제된 미소로 충분하고, 숨 들이키기와 움찔과 물러서기를 한꺼번에 시킬 필요는 없습니다.

실제 발췌 
Hinata expected the visitor at six o'clock.
She is surprised by the early arrival and is trying to remain polite while something unfinished inside the house distracts her.

This recognition interrupts her movement: she briefly stops, her upper eyelids lift slightly, and her lips part gently.

② 포즈 목록이 아니라 전환을 쓴다

미소가 언제 시작하고 누그러지고 사라지는지를 씁니다.

«미소를 띤다» 고만 적으면 첫 프레임부터 미소가 켜진 채 끝까지 갑니다. 표정이 붙어 있으면 사람이 아니라 가면처럼 보입니다.

반응에는 원인이 있어야 합니다. 방문자를 먼저 알아보고 나서 놀라고, 다시 말을 걸기 전에 예의를 회복하기 시작합니다. 새 정보를 처리하는 동안 표정이 그대로일 것을 요구하지 않습니다.

실제 발췌 
The smile develops across the movement rather than snapping on instantly.

The movements flow together without rigidly stopping in separate poses.

Her initial surprise softens into polite uncertainty.

③ 세기는 얼굴 단서로 정한다

금지 목록을 늘리는 것으로 원하는 연기를 대신할 수 없습니다.

«과장하지 마라» 를 열 줄 적어도 모델은 «그럼 어느 정도인가» 를 모릅니다. 빠진 것은 금지가 아니라 기준입니다.

그래서 보이는 것으로 적습니다. 편안한 볼, 살짝 올라간 입꼬리, 눈썹 안쪽의 작은 들림. 레퍼런스 사진이 활짝 웃는데 씬은 옅은 미소가 필요하다면, 그 사진을 빼거나 역할을 좁게 한정해야 합니다. 사진이 지시문을 이깁니다.

실제 발췌 
The smile is modest and a little effortful, not a broad grin.

She is slightly flustered and self-conscious, not frightened, angry, or suspicious.

Keep her cheeks relaxed and her smile restrained.

④ 대사는 낭독이 아니라 대화다

정확한 대사와 함께 «그 말이 대화에서 하는 일» 을 적습니다.

대사만 주고 시간을 넉넉히 잡으면 모델이 음절을 늘여 시간을 채웁니다. 그래서 또박또박 읽는 소리가 됩니다.

알아들었다는 표시인지, 조심스러운 확인인지, 혼잣말인지, 화제를 돌리려는 시도인지를 밝힙니다. 남는 시간은 문장 뒤에 배치합니다. 급한 대사는 «크게» 가 아니라 «빠른 대화 속도» 로 지정합니다.

실제 발췌 
Speak the exact Japanese sentence as one connected natural question.

Do not slow the speech to fill the time or insert long pauses between words.

The initial 「あ」 is a small realization. The rest is polite acknowledgment with a lightly questioning inflection.
Part Ⅲ

시선 · 침묵 · 동작

1인칭에서 렌즈는 상대의 눈입니다. 그래서 어디를 보는지와 언제 말을 멈추는지가 연기의 절반을 차지합니다.

⑤ 시선과 고개를 나눈다

눈만 움직이고 고개는 따라가지 않게 못 박습니다.

«아래를 본다» 고 적으면 고개가 함께 내려갑니다. 그러면 얼굴이 프레임에서 사라지고, 아래를 본 것이 아니라 고개를 숙인 것이 됩니다.

HER EYES ALONE travel downward toward the floor in front of you.
HER HEAD DOES NOT FOLLOW - the chin stays put; only eyelids and pupils move.

시선을 돌릴 때는 향할 구체적인 대상을 주고 자연스럽게 돌아오게 합니다. 카메라가 모든 시선을 따라갈 필요는 없습니다. 이렇게 하면 계속 응시하지 않아도 눈맞춤에 의미가 생깁니다.

실제 발췌 
When Hinata addresses the visitor, she looks directly into the camera lens.
When she addresses the man inside, she turns toward the interior, then returns to the lens afterward.

Her eyes shift toward the living-room direction inside, followed by a small head turn.

The camera stays on Hinata; do not pan or cut to the suitcase or clock.

⑥ 침묵도 연기다

5초 응답 구간은 정지 화면이 아니라 «듣고 있는 연기» 입니다.

«기다린다» 만 적으면 배우가 얼어붙거나, 반대로 침묵을 못 견뎌 끄덕임과 손짓을 계속 만들어냅니다. 둘 다 어색합니다.

필요한 것은 적은 양의 생기입니다. 조용한 미소, 이따금의 눈 깜빡임, 아주 작은 자세 변화. 그리고 배우는 아직 답을 듣지 못한 상태여야 합니다. «알아들었다» 는 반응은 5초가 끝난 뒤에 옵니다.

실제 발췌 
Occasional natural blinking, a slight softening around the eyes, and tiny relaxed posture adjustments keep her alive.

No freeze frame, slow motion, additional dialogue, silent mouthing, repeated nods, or exaggerated reactions.

Once the full five-second pause has ended, Hinata gives a small, natural nod as though she has heard the visitor's greeting.

⑦ 동작에 시작 · 접촉 · 끝을 준다

«그녀가 안에서 기다린다» 는 도착지를 말할 뿐, 어떻게 갔는지를 말하지 않습니다.

경로를 비워두면 인물이 순간이동합니다. 프레임 A 에서는 문 앞에 있고 프레임 B 에서는 마루에 있는데, 그 사이에 걸음이 없습니다.

그래서 경로와, 몸 · 손 · 물건의 관계를 적습니다. 위치가 바뀌는 순간에 카메라를 아래로 돌려 가리지 않습니다. 그리고 무엇이 남는지를 정합니다. 방문자가 손잡이를 놓으면 캐리어는 타일 위에 남아야 하고, 카메라를 따라오면 안 됩니다.

실제 발췌 
Keeping one hand on the door, she extends her free hand toward the hallway with an open, gently upward-facing palm.

Hinata releases the front door, which remains open.
She turns partly toward the interior and visibly takes two or three small steps across the tiled entrance, then steps onto the raised wooden floor.

The visitor parks the suitcase upright beside their left leg, clear of the front door's swing and the path onto the wooden floor, and releases the handle.

필요하면 동작을 겹칩니다. 말은 고개를 돌리는 도중이나 발을 딛는 끝에서 시작할 수 있고, 이게 과장 없이 급한 느낌을 만듭니다. 다만 무관한 동작 여러 개를 불가능한 시간에 밀어넣는 데 쓰지는 않습니다.

겹치기 — 실제 발췌 
Do not insert a pause between blocking the paper and beginning the line. The movement, head turn, and start of speech overlap naturally.
Part Ⅳ

카메라와 1인칭

1인칭이 깨지는 자리는 거의 정해져 있습니다. 카메라가 사람처럼 움직이지 않을 때, 그리고 손이 매번 다를 때입니다.

⑧ 카메라는 장비가 아니라 사람이다

프레임이 매끄럽게 미끄러지면 그 순간 «장비» 로 읽힙니다.

«앞으로 걸어간다» 만 적으면 모델은 달리 위에 얹은 것처럼 부드럽게 전진합니다. 사람은 그렇게 걷지 않습니다. 걸음마다 몸이 한 번 내려갔다 올라옵니다.

Each footstep lands with a small whole-frame DIP of a few centimetres that rises back by
the next step - once per step, the rhythm of a real person, not a shake. The frame
SWAYS a little as the weight shifts, and the gaze LEADS: the eyes turn first, the camera
follows a beat later. NOT a dolly, NOT a gimbal glide, NOT a steadicam drift.
If the frame moves smoothly like camera equipment, the frame is wrong.

카메라 움직임에는 물리적 이유가 있어야 합니다. 무언가를 살피려는 작은 고개 돌림, 들어가려는 짧은 전진, 마루에 오르는 완만한 상승. «시네마틱하게» 라는 이유만으로 카메라를 움직이지 않습니다.

고정 카메라 지시를 걷는 구간에 그대로 쓰지 않습니다.

대화 씬용 «움직이지 마라» 와 이동 씬용 «걸음마다 흔들려라» 는 정면으로 충돌합니다. 둘이 같은 프롬프트에 있으면 모델은 둘 중 하나를 버리는데, 어느 쪽을 버릴지는 알 수 없습니다.

고정 대화 · 걷기 — 실제 발췌 
[ 고정 대화 ]
Maintain the same eye-level conversational viewpoint as the starting frame.
No walking forward, zooming, deliberate nodding, or camera movement to follow Hinata's glances.
Keep Hinata's face and upper body readable within the vertical frame.

[ 걷기 · 이동 ]
Walking produces gentle footstep-induced bob and small irregular balance adjustments.
Hinata must visibly walk whenever her location changes.
Keep her in view throughout her movement from the doorway to the raised wooden floor. Show her crossing the tiled entrance and stepping onto the wood.
Her position changes through continuous walking, without skipped movement or sudden relocation.

⑨ 렌즈와 프레이밍은 숫자로 못 박는다

렌즈는 mm 가 아니라 대각 화각(도)과 눈높이(m)로 지정합니다. 숏 크기는 오직 거리로 정합니다.

«미디엄 숏» 은 프레임마다 다르게 해석됩니다. 그래서 같은 씬 안에서 인물 크기가 슬금슬금 변합니다. 줌으로 크기를 만들면 1인칭도 함께 깨집니다.

LENS - 65 degree diagonal field of view, eye level 1.55 m, axis level unless the beat
says otherwise. NO zoom, NO push-in, NO pull-out, NO focus pull. Shot size comes from
DISTANCE only.

프레이밍도 말이 아니라 화면에서 차지하는 비율로 적습니다.

HER SHOULDERS SPAN ABOUT FORTY PERCENT OF THE FRAME WIDTH and her head fills about
one seventh of the frame height, from the very first frame to the very last.
THE BOTTOM EDGE OF THE PICTURE CROSSES HER LEGS JUST BELOW THE KNEE.
If her whole body from head to toe comes into the picture, the shot is wrong.
한 화면에 전부 담으려 하지 않습니다.

천장과 인물 전신과 바닥과 손과 짐을 모두 넣으라고 하면, 모델은 렌즈를 넓혀서 해결합니다. 그 대가로 문틀과 벽선이 휩니다.

지붕 · 바닥 · 캐리어 · 하반신은 프레임 밖으로 나가도 됩니다. 그렇게 적어주는 것이 왜곡을 막는 가장 확실한 방법입니다.

⑩ 손은 매번 같은 손이어야 한다

1인칭에서 얼굴과 몸은 절대 보이지 않으므로, 손이 유일한 신체 신호입니다.

그런데 손은 그냥 두면 컷마다 달라집니다. 손가락이 붙거나 여섯 개가 되기도 합니다. 관객이 «AI 같다» 고 느끼는 대부분이 여기서 나옵니다.

그래서 손만은 인물 레퍼런스만큼 상세히 적습니다.

THE HAND IS THE SAME HAND IN EVERY SHOT: a young woman's right hand, slim
medium-length fingers, narrow wrist, small narrow back of the hand, tapered fingertips,
short bare nails, smooth skin, no standing tendons. No ring, polish, watch or bracelet.

Part Ⅴ

프롬프트 쓰는 법

같은 내용을 어떻게 적는지가 결과를 바꿉니다. 문장의 형태 자체가 지시입니다.

⑪ 금지가 아니라 실패 조건으로 쓴다

«하지 마라» 보다 «그렇게 되면 틀린 그림이다» 가 더 잘 듣습니다.

금지문은 모델에게 피할 것만 알려주고, 대신 무엇을 해야 하는지는 알려주지 않습니다. 실패 조건은 판정 기준을 주기 때문에 스스로 확인할 수 있습니다.

If the frame moves smoothly like camera equipment, the frame is wrong.
If her whole body from head to toe comes into the picture, the shot is wrong.

그리고 원하는 그림을 먼저 적습니다. 금지문은 그것을 보조할 때만 의미가 있습니다. 금지만 스무 줄 있는 프롬프트는 무엇을 원하는지 적지 않은 프롬프트입니다.

⑫ 된 것은 지키고, 하나씩 고친다

클립을 채택하면 그 실제 마지막 상태를 다음 시작 조건으로 씁니다.

문제를 두 개 같이 고치면, 결과가 나아지든 나빠지든 어느 쪽이 원인인지 알 수 없습니다. 프롬프트를 통째로 다시 쓰면 잘 되던 대사와 동선까지 잃습니다.

  1. 보이는 실패를 정확히 짚습니다 — 걷는 동작이 빠졌다, 미소가 너무 크다, 물건이 문턱을 넘었다, 문틀이 휘었다.
  2. 원인일 수 있는 지시나 빠진 전환을 찾습니다. 가설로 다룹니다.
  3. 그 지시만 고치고 모순되는 문구를 지웁니다.
  4. 다른 설정과 레퍼런스 역할은 가능한 한 그대로 둡니다.
  5. 새 결과에서 고쳐진 것과 의도치 않게 바뀐 것을 함께 봅니다.

소스에서 문이 닫혀 있으면, 연결본에서 열려 있다고 슬쩍 바꿔 쓰지 않습니다. 프롬프트가 길다고 좋거나 나쁘지도 않습니다. 일관된 지시, 명확한 물리적 순서, 가능한 타이밍이 글자 수보다 중요합니다.

Part Ⅵ

씬마다 정하는 것

고정 블록에 넣지 않고, 새 씬마다 따로 적습니다.

  • 정확한 대사와 화자. 오답 분기는 기본 생성에서 뺍니다.
  • 5초 응답 구간이 어디이고, 그동안 어떤 표정을 유지하는지
  • 인물의 시작 위치와 끝 위치
  • 보이는 이동 경로, 손과 물건의 접촉
  • 문 상태, 경첩 위치, 허용되는 움직임
  • 소품의 «주인» 과 그 뒤에 소품이 남는 자리
  • 다음 연결에 필요한 마지막 상태

이 프로젝트의 연속성 규칙

  • 방문자의 왼손은 민트색 캐리어를 끌고, 오른손으로 외부 인터폰을 누릅니다.
  • 방문자 소매는 흰색, 히나타 스웨터는 분홍색.
  • 캐리어는 현관 타일 바닥 왼쪽에 둡니다. 가운데 통로와 문이 열리는 궤적을 비웁니다.
  • 신발은 대본에 있을 때 화면 밖에서 벗습니다. 마루에 올라가는 것은 방문자뿐이고 캐리어는 남습니다.
  • 소스에 이미 보이는 건축을 유지합니다. 문 상태가 안 맞는다고 없던 문을 새로 만들지 않습니다.
Part Ⅶ

에셋

레퍼런스는 역할이 겹치지 않게 줍니다.

표정 사진과 의상 사진과 방 사진이 같은 대상을 두고 경쟁하면 안 됩니다.

레퍼런스를 많이 넣는다고 정확해지지 않습니다. 역할을 지정하지 않은 레퍼런스는 서로 다투다가 엉뚱한 조합을 만듭니다. 히나타의 분홍 스웨터가 1인칭 방문자에게 옮겨가는 식입니다.

실제로 쓴 레퍼런스 14장과, 각 이미지에 딸리는 지시문은 에셋 탭에 정리해 뒀습니다.

Part Ⅷ

하지 말 것

고정 블록에서 뺀 것

  • “breathing sway” — 카메라 움직임에 숨소리가 따라 붙습니다.
  • 옛 문 방향 — 현재 건축과 충돌합니다.

검증되지 않은 것

  • 노란 종이 씬은 아직 성공이 확인되지 않은 방향입니다.

원리적 한계

First-Person Japanese Drama
Production Guide

How to make a live-action drama where the camera is a character's eyes. “Natural” is not a matter of taste — it is the sum of things you can control. This guide splits those things into twelve.

Part Ⅰ

Getting started

Match the settings, pick a workflow, attach the fixed block. This part is the same every time.

Raising the bitrate does not improve the acting.

High only keeps the image from turning to mush. Whether the character reads as the same person, and whether the performance feels natural, is decided by the prompt and the references. Raising the bitrate for a soft image is right; raising it for awkward acting is wasted.

Two workflows

From a still

Continuing

The fixed block

This part stays the same across scenes. Do not mix actions, dialogue, or timing into it — attach those separately. Mixed in, you lose track of what to change on the next scene.

Full fixed first-person block 
ONE uninterrupted take, entirely from the first-person viewpoint of a new resident arriving at a Japanese share house. The camera IS the visitor's eyes. All movement happens in real time within one continuous physical space.

CAMERA AND FRAMING
Strict eye-level first-person viewpoint.
Maintain a fixed focal length throughout.

Door frames, roof edges, wall corners, and tile lines remain straight across the image.
Natural perspective convergence during head tilts is acceptable, but architectural lines must not bow or curve.

Frame only what is naturally visible from the visitor's position.
Allow the roof, ground, suitcase, and Hinata's lower body to extend outside the frame.
Do not widen the lens to fit everything into one image.

When stopped, retain very slight natural postural sway.
Camera movement must not bend, stretch, or warp the environment.
Never show the visitor's face or full body.

Camera movement is visual only and must not produce audible breathing.

CHARACTER PERFORMANCE
Whenever she addresses the visitor, she looks directly into the camera lens.
Play this with small, believable reactions rather than broad comedy.
Preserve her normal facial proportions.
No exaggerated wide eyes, stretched mouth, or theatrical shock.

GLOBAL INTERACTIVE AUDIO RULE
The visitor NEVER produces audible dialogue, breathing, sighs, gasps, humming, laughter, or other vocal sounds.
Natural camera movement is visual only and must not be accompanied by breath noise.
Footsteps, suitcase wheels, and physical interaction sounds are allowed.

Whenever the script reaches a visitor response, replace the spoken response with approximately FIVE SECONDS of the other character looking into the lens and naturally waiting or listening.
No audible visitor answer, subtitles, response text, or countdown.

Use occasional natural blinking and tiny relaxed posture adjustments.
Her mouth remains relaxed and still.
She does not speak again or react as though an answer has already been confirmed.

CONTINUOUS ACTION
These timestamps are successive beats within ONE uninterrupted take, not separate shots.
Preserve all actions in order.
Use normal-speed movement with connected transitions between actions.

FINAL CONSTRAINTS
No cuts, hidden cuts, transitions, montage, reverse angles, inserts, time jumps, slow motion, or speed ramps.
No subtitles, captions, speech bubbles, countdowns, logos, or text overlays.
The most common mistake with this block

“Her mouth remains relaxed and still” and “she does not react as though an answer has been confirmed” apply during the five-second response window only, not during dialogue.

Applied to the whole scene, the actor freezes mid-sentence. If you need an acknowledgment, write it separately after the five seconds. The accepted entrance flow put a small nod and 「はい！あ、どうぞ。」 there.

Part Ⅱ

Writing performance

Naming an emotion does not define the performance. Give only “surprised” and the model decides how big the surprise is — usually bigger than you wanted.

① Give a reason, then specify the visible response

Write what the character expected, what changed, and what they are trying to hide.

A single emotion label leaves most of the performance undefined. The model invents whatever you left out, and what it invents is what reads as overacting.

So give it an order: expectation → trigger → small physical reaction → social recovery. Do not stack several strong emotions on one moment. Mild embarrassment reads through a hesitant gaze and a restrained smile; it does not need a gasp and a flinch and a step back all at once.

Actual excerpt 
Hinata expected the visitor at six o'clock.
She is surprised by the early arrival and is trying to remain polite while something unfinished inside the house distracts her.

This recognition interrupts her movement: she briefly stops, her upper eyelids lift slightly, and her lips part gently.

② Write transitions, not a list of poses

Write when the smile begins, softens, and fades.

Write only “she smiles” and the smile is switched on from the first frame and stays. A fixed expression reads as a mask, not a person.

Let a reaction have a cause: she notices the visitor before becoming surprised, and begins recovering her politeness before speaking again. Do not require an expression to hold steady while the character processes new information.

Actual excerpt 
The smile develops across the movement rather than snapping on instantly.

The movements flow together without rigidly stopping in separate poses.

Her initial surprise softens into polite uncertainty.

③ Set intensity with facial cues

A longer list of prohibitions cannot stand in for the performance you want.

Ten lines of “do not exaggerate” still leave the model without an answer to “then how much?” What is missing is not a prohibition but a reference point.

So write what is visible: relaxed cheeks, slightly raised mouth corners, a small lift of the inner eyebrows. If a reference portrait grins broadly but the scene needs a faint smile, omit that portrait or narrow its role. The image beats the sentence.

Actual excerpt 
The smile is modest and a little effortful, not a broad grin.

She is slightly flustered and self-conscious, not frightened, angry, or suspicious.

Keep her cheeks relaxed and her smile restrained.

④ Dialogue is a conversation, not a recital

Write the exact line together with the job that line does in the conversation.

Give the line and a generous time budget, and the model stretches syllables to fill the time. That is what makes it sound recited.

Say whether it is acknowledgment, cautious confirmation, a quiet aside, or an attempt to change the subject. Put spare time after the sentence. When a line needs urgency, specify brisk conversational delivery rather than more volume.

Actual excerpt 
Speak the exact Japanese sentence as one connected natural question.

Do not slow the speech to fill the time or insert long pauses between words.

The initial 「あ」 is a small realization. The rest is polite acknowledgment with a lightly questioning inflection.
Part Ⅲ

Gaze · silence · action

In first person the lens is the other person's eyes. So where someone looks, and when they stop talking, carry half the performance.

⑤ Split the eyes from the head

Pin the eyes to move while the head explicitly does not follow.

Write “she looks down” and the head goes down with it. The face leaves the frame, and what you get is not a glance downward but a bowed head.

HER EYES ALONE travel downward toward the floor in front of you.
HER HEAD DOES NOT FOLLOW - the chin stays put; only eyelids and pupils move.

When the gaze leaves, give it a specific thing to land on and bring it back naturally. The camera need not follow every glance. Done this way, eye contact means something without constant staring.

Actual excerpt 
When Hinata addresses the visitor, she looks directly into the camera lens.
When she addresses the man inside, she turns toward the interior, then returns to the lens afterward.

Her eyes shift toward the living-room direction inside, followed by a small head turn.

The camera stays on Hinata; do not pan or cut to the suitcase or clock.

⑥ Silence is performance too

The five-second response window is not a frozen frame — it is the performance of listening.

Write only “she waits” and the actor either locks up or, unable to bear the silence, manufactures nods and gestures to fill it. Both read wrong.

What you need is a small amount of life: a quiet smile, the occasional blink, a tiny postural shift. And the actor must not have heard an answer yet. Acknowledgment comes after the five seconds.

Actual excerpt 
Occasional natural blinking, a slight softening around the eyes, and tiny relaxed posture adjustments keep her alive.

No freeze frame, slow motion, additional dialogue, silent mouthing, repeated nods, or exaggerated reactions.

Once the full five-second pause has ended, Hinata gives a small, natural nod as though she has heard the visitor's greeting.

⑦ Give each action a start, contact, and end

“She waits inside” names a destination; it does not say how she got there.

Leave the route empty and the character teleports. In one frame she is at the door, in the next she is on the wooden floor, and there are no steps in between.

So write the route, and the relationship between body, hands, and objects. Do not turn the camera away at the exact moment a position changes. And decide what stays behind: once the visitor releases the handle, the suitcase must remain on the tile rather than follow the camera.

Actual excerpt 
Keeping one hand on the door, she extends her free hand toward the hallway with an open, gently upward-facing palm.

Hinata releases the front door, which remains open.
She turns partly toward the interior and visibly takes two or three small steps across the tiled entrance, then steps onto the raised wooden floor.

The visitor parks the suitcase upright beside their left leg, clear of the front door's swing and the path onto the wooden floor, and releases the handle.

Overlap actions when it helps. Speech can begin during a head turn or as a step finishes, and that creates urgency without exaggeration. Do not use overlap to force unrelated actions into an impossible window.

Overlap — actual excerpt 
Do not insert a pause between blocking the paper and beginning the line. The movement, head turn, and start of speech overlap naturally.
Part Ⅳ

Camera and POV

First person breaks in predictable places: when the camera does not move like a person, and when the hand is different every time.

⑧ The camera is a person, not a rig

The moment the frame glides smoothly, it reads as equipment.

Write only “walk forward” and the model moves forward smoothly, as if on a dolly. People do not walk like that. The body drops and rises once per step.

Each footstep lands with a small whole-frame DIP of a few centimetres that rises back by
the next step - once per step, the rhythm of a real person, not a shake. The frame
SWAYS a little as the weight shifts, and the gaze LEADS: the eyes turn first, the camera
follows a beat later. NOT a dolly, NOT a gimbal glide, NOT a steadicam drift.
If the frame moves smoothly like camera equipment, the frame is wrong.

Camera movement needs physical motivation: a small head turn to investigate, a short move forward to enter, a modest rise to step onto a raised floor. Do not move the camera because a scene is described as cinematic.

Do not apply stationary-camera instructions to a walking segment.

“Do not move” for a conversation and “bob with every step” for a relocation are in direct conflict. With both in one prompt the model drops one of them, and you do not get to choose which.

Stationary · walking — actual excerpts 
[ Stationary conversation ]
Maintain the same eye-level conversational viewpoint as the starting frame.
No walking forward, zooming, deliberate nodding, or camera movement to follow Hinata's glances.
Keep Hinata's face and upper body readable within the vertical frame.

[ Walking and relocation ]
Walking produces gentle footstep-induced bob and small irregular balance adjustments.
Hinata must visibly walk whenever her location changes.
Keep her in view throughout her movement from the doorway to the raised wooden floor. Show her crossing the tiled entrance and stepping onto the wood.
Her position changes through continuous walking, without skipped movement or sudden relocation.

⑨ Pin lens and framing with numbers

Specify the lens as a diagonal field of view in degrees and an eye height in metres. Shot size comes from distance only.

“Medium shot” gets interpreted differently frame to frame, so the character drifts in size within one scene. Sizing with zoom breaks the first person along with it.

LENS - 65 degree diagonal field of view, eye level 1.55 m, axis level unless the beat
says otherwise. NO zoom, NO push-in, NO pull-out, NO focus pull. Shot size comes from
DISTANCE only.

Write framing as a share of the frame rather than as a word.

HER SHOULDERS SPAN ABOUT FORTY PERCENT OF THE FRAME WIDTH and her head fills about
one seventh of the frame height, from the very first frame to the very last.
THE BOTTOM EDGE OF THE PICTURE CROSSES HER LEGS JUST BELOW THE KNEE.
If her whole body from head to toe comes into the picture, the shot is wrong.
Do not try to fit everything into one frame.

Ask for the ceiling and the full body and the floor and the hands and the luggage, and the model solves it by widening the lens. The price is bowed door frames and wall lines.

The roof, the ground, the suitcase, and the lower body are allowed to leave the frame. Saying so is the most reliable way to prevent distortion.

⑩ The hand must be the same hand every time

The face and body are never visible in first person, so the hand is your only body signal.

Left unspecified, the hand changes shot to shot. Fingers fuse, or a sixth one appears. Most of what makes viewers say “this looks AI” comes from here.

So describe the hand as carefully as a character reference.

THE HAND IS THE SAME HAND IN EVERY SHOT: a young woman's right hand, slim
medium-length fingers, narrow wrist, small narrow back of the hand, tapered fingertips,
short bare nails, smooth skin, no standing tendons. No ring, polish, watch or bracelet.
FIVE SEPARATE FINGERS with clear shadow gaps - four fingers plus one thumb, correct
anatomy, no fused or melted fingers.
Part Ⅴ

Writing the prompt

How you phrase the same content changes the result. The shape of the sentence is itself an instruction.

⑪ Write the failure condition, not the prohibition

“If it looks like X, the shot is wrong” lands better than “do not do X”.

A prohibition tells the model only what to avoid, never what to do instead. A failure condition supplies a test, so the model can check itself against it.

If the frame moves smoothly like camera equipment, the frame is wrong.
If her whole body from head to toe comes into the picture, the shot is wrong.

And describe the wanted image first. Prohibitions only help in support of that. A prompt with twenty lines of prohibition is a prompt that never said what it wanted.

⑫ Preserve what worked; fix one thing

When a clip is accepted, use its actual final state as the next starting condition.

Fix two problems at once and, better or worse, you cannot tell which change did it. Rewrite the prompt wholesale and you lose the dialogue and staging that were already working.

  1. Identify the visible failure precisely: missing walking steps, an overly broad smile, an object crossing the threshold, a bowed door frame.
  2. Find the instruction or omitted transition that could be contributing. Treat it as a hypothesis.
  3. Change only that, and remove contradictory wording.
  4. Keep other settings and reference roles stable where practical.
  5. Inspect the new result for both the correction and unintended changes.

If the source door is closed, do not quietly describe it as open in the continuation. And prompt length is not itself good or bad — consistent instructions, clear physical sequences, and achievable timing matter more than word count.

Part Ⅵ

Decide per scene

Keep these out of the fixed block and write them fresh each time.

  • Exact spoken lines and their speakers. Exclude incorrect-answer branches from the default generation.
  • Where the five-second response window occurs and what expression is held during it.
  • Character starting and ending positions.
  • Visible walking routes and hand–object contact.
  • Door state, hinge location, permitted movement.
  • Prop ownership and where props remain afterward.
  • The final state needed for the next continuation.

Current project continuity

  • Visitor's left hand pulls the mint suitcase; right hand presses the exterior intercom.
  • Visitor sleeves are white; Hinata's sweater is pink.
  • The suitcase stays on the lower entrance tiles, toward the left, clear of the central path and door swing.
  • Shoes are removed offscreen when scripted. Only the visitor steps onto the wooden floor; the suitcase stays behind.
  • Preserve architecture already visible in the source. Do not invent another door to repair an inconsistent door state.
Part Ⅶ

Assets

Give references roles that do not overlap.

An expression portrait, a clothing image, and a room image must not compete to define the same subject.

More references do not mean more accuracy. References without assigned roles fight each other and produce wrong combinations — Hinata's pink sweater migrating onto the first-person visitor, for instance.

Part Ⅷ

What not to do

Do not reuse something merely because it appeared in a preferred prompt.

Removed from the fixed block

  • An audible visitor reply — now replaced by the five-second silence.
  • “breathing sway” — breath noise follows the camera movement.
  • The older door orientation — it conflicts with the current architecture.

Not validated

  • The 40mm instruction is not the fix for distortion. The reusable block keeps framing and straight-line instructions without mandating a focal length.
  • The yellow-paper scene is a direction not yet confirmed successful.

Inherent limits

Preservation language is a request to the model, not a pixel lock. If an ending must remain exactly unchanged, retain that original footage in the final edit and inspect the join.

Keep purposeful actions brief, but allow enough time to show movement. Do not fill 15 seconds with unnecessary pauses, or compress walking until characters appear to teleport. The five-second interaction window is intentional; other pauses should serve the scene.
