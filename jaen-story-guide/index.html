1. 이 문서의 근거

채택한 영상은 모두 Seedance 2.5 로 만들었습니다. 아래 설정은 대화 기록과 화면 캡처에서 정리한 것이고, 채택본 하나하나의 생성 메타데이터를 전부 복원한 것은 아닙니다. 에셋 선택 지침은 작업 흐름을 종합한 것입니다.

인용한 지시문과 재사용 프롬프트 블록은 이전에 실제로 쓴 프롬프트에서 발췌해 조합한 것입니다. 모든 문장이 모든 채택본에 들어 있었다는 뜻은 아닙니다.

확정 아님

노란 종이 씬은 아직 검증되지 않은 새 방향입니다. 성공 사례가 아닙니다.

이전 채택본에는 19초짜리도 있습니다. 15초는 지금의 작업 단위일 뿐, 지켜야 하는 규격이 아닙니다.

확립된 워크플로 둘

  • Start-frame 생성 — 스틸로 구도를 먼저 확정하고 그 상태에서 움직입니다. 왜곡이 가장 적었던 채택본의 프롬프트에는 이 문장이 명시돼 있었습니다: “Continue directly from the supplied starting frame.”
  • Forward 확장 — 채택한 클립의 실제 마지막 프레임에서 잇습니다. 입장 씬 03_1.mp4 가 이 방식으로 나왔습니다.

어느 쪽도 연속성을 보장하지 않습니다. 움직임·대사·인물 동일성·이음매를 눈으로 확인하고 채택하세요. 비트레이트 High 는 압축 손실을 줄일 뿐, 연기나 인물 동일성을 잡아주지 않습니다.

3. 실측으로 확인한 함정 셋

생성 기록을 직접 조회해 발견한 것들입니다. 원 가이드에는 없던 내용입니다.

함정 1 — 길이는 파라미터가 이긴다

프롬프트에 Create an 8-second… 라고 써도 duration 파라미터가 15면 15초짜리가 나옵니다. 반대로 Create a 15-second… 라고 쓰고 파라미터를 11로 두면 11초가 나옵니다. 실제로 두 경우 다 기록에 있습니다.

프롬프트 안의 타임스탬프(0.0–1.0, 4.0–7.5)는 전부 그 길이 기준으로 다시 계산해야 합니다. 안 맞추면 대사가 잘리거나 뒤가 빕니다.

고칠 자리는 세 곳입니다. ①파라미터 duration ②프롬프트 첫 줄의 “N-second” ③비트 타임스탬프. 셋을 항상 같이 고칩니다.

함정 2 — 확장 모드에서는 화면비 파라미터가 무의미하다

Forward / Backward 확장과 영상 편집 작업의 기록을 보면 aspect_ratio 가 16:9 로 찍혀 있는데, 실제 출력은 480×854 (9:16) 입니다. 확장·편집 모드에서는 소스 영상의 비율을 따라가고 파라미터는 무시됩니다.

그러니 파라미터 표시를 보고 «세로가 아니네» 하고 당황하지 않아도 됩니다. 대신 진짜로 확인할 것은 소스 영상 자체가 9:16 인가 입니다. 소스가 가로면 결과도 가로입니다.

함정 3 — 프롬프트 칸에 대화가 섞여 들어간다

기록에 프롬프트 본문이 맞아요. 제가 임의로 히나타 연기만 테스트하는 장면으로 줄이면서… 로 시작하는 생성이 3건 연속으로 남아 있습니다. 설명하려고 쓴 한국어 문장이 그대로 프롬프트로 들어간 것입니다.

모델은 이걸 지시로 읽습니다. 프롬프트 칸에는 영어 지시문만 넣고, 붙여넣기 전에 첫 줄이 Create a… 또는 Extend the… 로 시작하는지 눈으로 봅니다.

4. 에셋 체크리스트

에셋쓸모 있으려면실제로 쓴 지시문
씬 시작 프레임의도한 시점, 곧은 건축선, 인물 위치 정확, 원치 않는 손·물건 없음 “Match the starting frame's camera position, framing, Hinata's facial identity, hairstyle, clothing, door opening angle, hand placement, lighting, and entrance layout.”
인물 동일성 레퍼런스또렷한 얼굴 정면, 옷·비율용 전신 “Use the detailed facial portrait for facial identity and the full-body view for clothing and proportions.”
표정 레퍼런스의도한 연기에 가까운 절제된 표정 “Do not hold the reference expression as a fixed pose or exaggerate the size of her eyes.”
실내 레퍼런스문턱·바닥 단차·문·계단·가구가 일관 “Do not mirror or rearrange the layout.” / “This image defines the interior space, not a separate shot.”
실외 레퍼런스이번 씬에 필요한 건축만. 와이드 구도를 강요하지 않을 것 “Use the architecture as reference, not the photograph's wide composition.”
방문자 의상소매 원단·색·소맷단이 읽힐 것 “Use ONLY for the unseen first-person visitor's white cotton sweatshirt sleeves and ribbed cuffs.”
소품형태·색·손잡이가 또렷할 것 “Only the handle and a small portion of the case need to enter the lower-left edge.” / “Do not force the whole suitcase into view.”
채택한 소스 영상이어갈 만한 끝 자세·카메라 위치·움직임·씬 상태 “Begin directly from the source video's actual final frame and continue its ongoing movement naturally.”

레퍼런스는 역할을 겹치지 않게 줍니다. 표정 사진과 의상 사진과 방 사진이 같은 대상을 두고 경쟁하면 안 됩니다. 이번 씬에 필요한 것만 넣습니다. 실내만 보이는 연결 씬에 집 전경 사진은 필요 없습니다.

실측

레퍼런스 개수는 작업이 진행되면서 2 → 3 → 4 → 7 → 8장으로 늘었습니다. 많이 넣는다고 좋아지지 않습니다. 늘어난 만큼 역할 배정 문장도 같이 늘려야 합니다.

레퍼런스 역할 분리 — 실제 발췌

All three images depict ONE AND THE SAME woman. Show only one Hinata.

Keep each reference assigned to its specified subject.
Do not reproduce studio backgrounds, reference-sheet layouts, duplicate portraits, or split screens.

Hinata's references apply ONLY to the woman inside the doorway.
Do not transfer her pink sweater to the first-person visitor.

Do not generate this reference person standing in the scene.
Do not copy the reference person's face, hairstyle, or full-body pose into the video.

첫 줄은 히나타 이미지가 3장이라는 전제이고, 현관 문구는 그 씬에만 해당합니다. 실제 레퍼런스 수와 씬에 맞게 고쳐 쓰고, 그대로 복사하지 않습니다.

5. 고정 1인칭 프롬프트 블록

이전 프롬프트에서 실제로 쓴 문장들을 모은 블록입니다. 씬의 동작·정확한 대사·타이밍은 따로 붙입니다. 셰어하우스 도착 설정은 이 프로젝트 전용입니다.

ONE uninterrupted take, entirely from the first-person viewpoint of a new resident arriving at a Japanese share house. The camera IS the visitor's eyes. All movement happens in real time within one continuous physical space.

CAMERA AND FRAMING
Strict eye-level first-person viewpoint.
Maintain a fixed focal length throughout.

Door frames, roof edges, wall corners, and tile lines remain straight across the image.
Natural perspective convergence during head tilts is acceptable, but architectural lines must not bow or curve.

Frame only what is naturally visible from the visitor's position.
Allow the roof, ground, suitcase, and Hinata's lower body to extend outside the frame.
Do not widen the lens to fit everything into one image.

When stopped, retain very slight natural postural sway.
Camera movement must not bend, stretch, or warp the environment.
Never show the visitor's face or full body.

Camera movement is visual only and must not produce audible breathing.

CHARACTER PERFORMANCE
Whenever she addresses the visitor, she looks directly into the camera lens.
Play this with small, believable reactions rather than broad comedy.
Preserve her normal facial proportions.
No exaggerated wide eyes, stretched mouth, or theatrical shock.

GLOBAL INTERACTIVE AUDIO RULE
The visitor NEVER produces audible dialogue, breathing, sighs, gasps, humming, laughter, or other vocal sounds.
Natural camera movement is visual only and must not be accompanied by breath noise.
Footsteps, suitcase wheels, and physical interaction sounds are allowed.

Whenever the script reaches a visitor response, replace the spoken response with approximately FOUR SECONDS of the other character looking into the lens and naturally waiting or listening.
No audible visitor answer, subtitles, response text, or countdown.

Use occasional natural blinking and tiny relaxed posture adjustments.
Her mouth remains relaxed and still.
She does not speak again or react as though an answer has already been confirmed.

CONTINUOUS ACTION
These timestamps are successive beats within ONE uninterrupted take, not separate shots.
Preserve all actions in order.
Use normal-speed movement with connected transitions between actions.

FINAL CONSTRAINTS
No cuts, hidden cuts, transitions, montage, reverse angles, inserts, time jumps, slow motion, or speed ramps.
No subtitles, captions, speech bubbles, countdowns, logos, or text overlays.
적용 범위 주의

«입을 편히 다물고 있다», «대답을 들은 것처럼 굴지 않는다» 는 4초 응답 구간에만 적용됩니다. 대사 중에는 아닙니다.

다음 동작이 «알아들었다» 는 반응이라면 4초 뒤에 명시적으로 배치합니다. 채택된 입장 씬은 응답 구간 뒤에 작은 끄덕임과 「はい！あ、どうぞ。」 를 넣었습니다.

6. 시작 블록 고르기

Start image — 실제 발췌

Create a 15-second vertical 9:16 photorealistic Japanese live-action drama with synchronized Japanese dialogue and realistic location sound.

Continue directly from the supplied starting frame.

Match the starting frame's camera position, framing, Hinata's facial identity, hairstyle, clothing, door opening angle, hand placement, lighting, and entrance layout.

Extend Video — 실제 발췌

Extend the uploaded video FORWARD with a 15-second continuation in vertical 9:16.

Begin directly from the source video's actual final frame and continue its ongoing movement naturally. Preserve Hinata's position, pose, gaze direction, door angle, camera position, framing, lighting, and visible environment.

Maintain one uninterrupted first-person shot from the visitor's eye level. Do not restart the scene, replay preceding actions, or repeat dialogue from the source video.

All timestamps below refer only to the newly added 15-second continuation.

Forward 확장에서는 채택한 소스 영상만 올리고, 특별히 끝점을 지정할 이유가 없으면 Start / End 이미지는 넣지 않습니다. End 레퍼런스를 넣는다고 끝 구간이 원본과 똑같이 남는다는 보장은 없습니다.

실측 — 잘 먹힌 표기

시작 프레임을 확실히 물리려면 이 한 줄이 효과적이었습니다.

@Image 1 IS THE FIRST FRAME. The shot begins exactly on it - same framing,
same objects in the same places, same light. Nothing is re-staged or added at the start.

7. 움직임 지시

필요할 때만 넣습니다.

고정 대화 — 왜곡이 가장 적었던 채택본 발췌

Maintain the same eye-level conversational viewpoint as the starting frame.

No walking forward, zooming, deliberate nodding, or camera movement to follow Hinata's glances.

Keep Hinata's face and upper body readable within the vertical frame.

걷기·이동 — 입장 씬 프롬프트 발췌

Walking produces gentle footstep-induced bob and small irregular balance adjustments.

Hinata must visibly walk whenever her location changes.
Keep her in view throughout her movement from the doorway to the raised wooden floor. Show her crossing the tiled entrance and stepping onto the wood.
Her position changes through continuous walking, without skipped movement or sudden relocation.

고정 카메라 지시를 걷는 구간에 그대로 쓰지 않습니다. 현관→마루 경로는 나중 씬에서 실제 경로로 바꿔 씁니다.

실측 — 걷는 카메라를 «장비가 아닌 사람»으로

1인칭에서 카메라가 미끄러지면 장비처럼 보입니다. 실제로 쓴 문장:

Each footstep lands with a small whole-frame DIP of a few centimetres that rises back by
the next step - once per step, the rhythm of a real person, not a shake. The frame
SWAYS a little as the weight shifts, and the gaze LEADS: the eyes turn first, the camera
follows a beat later. NOT a dolly, NOT a gimbal glide, NOT a steadicam drift.
If the frame moves smoothly like camera equipment, the frame is wrong.

마지막 줄의 형태에 주목하세요. If X, the frame is wrong. «하지 마라» 보다 «그러면 틀린 것이다» 가 더 잘 듣습니다.

8. 씬마다 따로 쓸 것

  • 정확한 대사와 화자. 오답 분기는 기본 생성에서 뺍니다.
  • 4초 응답 구간이 어디이고, 그동안 어떤 표정을 유지하는지
  • 인물의 시작 위치와 끝 위치
  • 보이는 이동 경로, 손과 물건의 접촉
  • 문 상태, 경첩 위치, 허용되는 움직임
  • 소품의 «주인» 과 그 뒤에 소품이 남는 자리
  • 다음 연결에 필요한 마지막 상태

이 프로젝트의 연속성 규칙

  • 방문자의 왼손은 민트색 캐리어를 끌고, 오른손으로 외부 인터폰을 누릅니다.
  • 방문자 소매는 흰색, 히나타 스웨터는 분홍색.
  • 캐리어는 현관 타일 바닥 왼쪽에 둡니다. 가운데 통로와 문이 열리는 궤적을 비웁니다.
  • 신발은 대본에 있을 때 화면 밖에서 벗습니다. 마루에 올라가는 것은 방문자뿐이고 캐리어는 남습니다.
  • 소스에 이미 보이는 건축을 유지합니다. 문 상태가 안 맞는다고 없던 문을 새로 만들지 않습니다.

9. 자연스러운 연기를 만드는 법

결과 연기와 그림이 «아주 자연스러웠다» 고 평가된 프롬프트들이 어떻게 쓰였는지 정리한 것입니다. 이 프로젝트에서 끌어낸 실무 원칙이고, 모델 성능의 원인을 실험으로 분리한 것은 아닙니다. 어떤 문구도 결과를 보장하지 않습니다.

① 배우에게 «그럴 이유» 를 주고, 보이는 반응을 지정한다

감정 이름만 주면 연기의 대부분이 미정으로 남습니다. 무엇을 예상했고, 무엇이 달라졌고, 무엇을 감추려 하는지를 정한 다음 작고 관찰 가능한 반응 몇 개를 적습니다.

Hinata expected the visitor at six o'clock.
She is surprised by the early arrival and is trying to remain polite while something unfinished inside the house distracts her.

This recognition interrupts her movement: she briefly stops, her upper eyelids lift slightly, and her lips part gently.

패턴은 기대 → 계기 → 작은 신체 반응 → 사회적 수습 입니다. 한 순간에 강한 감정을 여러 개 쌓지 않습니다. 가벼운 민망함은 머뭇거리는 시선과 절제된 미소로 충분하고, 숨 들이키기·움찔·크게 물러서기를 동시에 시킬 필요가 없습니다.

② 포즈 목록이 아니라 «표정의 전환» 을 쓴다

자연스러운 연기는 움직임과 말 위에서 전개됩니다. 하나의 표정을 씬 내내 유지시키지 말고, 미소가 언제 시작하고 누그러지고 사라지는지를 씁니다.

The smile develops across the movement rather than snapping on instantly.

The movements flow together without rigidly stopping in separate poses.

Her initial surprise softens into polite uncertainty.

반응에는 원인이 있어야 합니다. 방문자를 먼저 알아보고 놀라고, 다시 말을 걸기 전에 예의를 회복하기 시작합니다. 새 정보를 처리하는 동안 미소가 그대로일 것을 요구하지 않습니다.

③ 세기는 «몇 개의 얼굴 단서» 로 지정한다

The smile is modest and a little effortful, not a broad grin.

She is slightly flustered and self-conscious, not frightened, angry, or suspicious.

Keep her cheeks relaxed and her smile restrained.

쓸모 있는 지시는 보이는 연기 그 자체입니다. 금지 표정을 길게 나열하는 것으로 대신할 수 없습니다. 레퍼런스 사진이 활짝 웃는데 씬은 옅은 미소가 필요하다면, 그 사진을 빼거나 역할을 좁게 한정합니다.

④ 일본어를 «낭독» 이 아니라 «대화» 로 연출한다

Speak the exact Japanese sentence as one connected natural question.

Do not slow the speech to fill the time or insert long pauses between words.

The initial 「あ」 is a small realization. The rest is polite acknowledgment with a lightly questioning inflection.

정확한 대사를 적고 그 말의 대화적 목적을 설명합니다 — 알아들었다는 표시인지, 조심스러운 확인인지, 혼잣말인지, 화제를 돌리려는 시도인지. 남는 시간은 문장 뒤에 배치하지, 음절을 늘여서 채우지 않습니다. 급한 대사는 «크게» 가 아니라 «빠른 대화 속도» 로 지정합니다.

⑤ 시선에 목적을 주고, 듣는 쪽도 반응하게 한다

When Hinata addresses the visitor, she looks directly into the camera lens.
When she addresses the man inside, she turns toward the interior, then returns to the lens afterward.

Her eyes shift toward the living-room direction inside, followed by a small head turn.

The camera stays on Hinata; do not pan or cut to the suitcase or clock.
실측 — 시선과 고개를 분리한다

가장 «사람 같다» 는 평을 받은 문장 구조입니다. 눈만 움직이고 고개는 따라가지 않게 못 박습니다.

HER EYES ALONE travel downward toward the floor in front of you.
HER HEAD DOES NOT FOLLOW - the chin stays put; only eyelids and pupils move.

⑥ 응답 구간을 «적극적 듣기» 로 다룬다

4초 공백은 상호작용의 일부이지 정지 화면이 아닙니다.

Occasional natural blinking, a slight softening around the eyes, and tiny relaxed posture adjustments keep her alive.

No freeze frame, slow motion, additional dialogue, silent mouthing, repeated nods, or exaggerated reactions.

Once the full four-second pause has ended, Hinata gives a small, natural nod as though she has heard the visitor's greeting.

침묵을 메우려고 잦은 끄덕임·반복적인 시선 이동·손짓을 요구하지 않습니다. 조용한 미소와 이따금의 눈 깜빡임으로 충분합니다.

⑦ 모든 동작에 시작·접촉·끝을 준다

«그녀가 안에서 기다린다» 는 도착지를 말할 뿐, 어떻게 갔는지를 설명하지 않습니다.

Keeping one hand on the door, she extends her free hand toward the hallway with an open, gently upward-facing palm.

Hinata releases the front door, which remains open.
She turns partly toward the interior and visibly takes two or three small steps across the tiled entrance, then steps onto the raised wooden floor.

The visitor parks the suitcase upright beside their left leg, clear of the front door's swing and the path onto the wooden floor, and releases the handle.

중요한 위치 변화는 보이게 둡니다. 인물이 이동하는 바로 그 순간에 카메라를 아래로 틸트해 가리지 않습니다. 그리고 무엇이 남는지를 정합니다 — 방문자가 손잡이를 놓으면 캐리어는 타일 위에 남지, 카메라를 따라오지 않습니다.

⑧ 필요하면 동작을 겹친다

Do not insert a pause between blocking the paper and beginning the line. The movement, head turn, and start of speech overlap naturally.

말은 고개를 돌리는 도중이나 발을 딛는 끝에서 시작할 수 있습니다. 이게 과장 없이 급박함을 만듭니다. 다만 이 종이 씬 지시는 아직 성공이 확인되지 않았습니다. 겹치기는 분명한 의도를 표현할 때 쓰고, 무관한 동작 여러 개를 불가능한 시간에 밀어넣는 데 쓰지 않습니다.

⑨ 카메라 움직임에 «물리적 이유» 를 준다

The upward look and left-right glances are small physical head rotations.

Keep them compact and human, not sweeping cinematic pans.

Frame only what is naturally visible from the visitor's position.
Do not widen the lens to fit everything into one image.

무언가를 살피려는 작은 고개 돌림, 들어가려는 짧은 전진, 마루에 오르는 완만한 상승. «시네마틱» 이라는 이유만으로 카메라를 움직이지 않습니다.

실측 — 화각과 눈높이를 숫자로

렌즈를 mm 가 아니라 대각 화각(도)과 눈높이(m)로 지정했습니다.

LENS - 65 degree diagonal field of view, eye level 1.55 m, axis level unless the beat
says otherwise. NO zoom, NO push-in, NO pull-out, NO focus pull. Shot size comes from
DISTANCE only.

«숏 크기는 오직 거리로 정해진다» 가 핵심입니다. 줌으로 크기를 만들면 1인칭이 깨집니다.

실측 — 프레이밍을 비율로 못 박는다

«미디엄 숏» 같은 말 대신 화면에서 차지하는 비율로 지정했습니다.

HER SHOULDERS SPAN ABOUT FORTY PERCENT OF THE FRAME WIDTH and her head fills about
one seventh of the frame height, from the very first frame to the very last.
THE BOTTOM EDGE OF THE PICTURE CROSSES HER LEGS JUST BELOW THE KNEE.
If her whole body from head to toe comes into the picture, the shot is wrong.
실측 — 1인칭에서 손은 따로 규격을 준다

화면에 들어오는 손은 매번 같아야 합니다. 손가락 붕괴도 여기서 막습니다.

THE HAND IS THE SAME HAND IN EVERY SHOT: a young woman's right hand, slim
medium-length fingers, narrow wrist, small narrow back of the hand, tapered fingertips,
short bare nails, smooth skin, no standing tendons. No ring, polish, watch or bracelet.
FIVE SEPARATE FINGERS with clear shadow gaps - four fingers plus one thumb, correct
anatomy, no fused or melted fingers.

⑩ 된 것은 지키고, 문제는 한 번에 하나씩 고친다

클립을 채택하면 그 실제 마지막 상태를 다음 시작 조건으로 씁니다. 작은 문제를 고칠 때 성공한 대사와 동선은 그대로 둡니다. 소스에서 문이 닫혀 있으면, 연결본에서 열려 있다고 슬쩍 바꿔 쓰지 않습니다.

  1. 보이는 실패를 정확히 짚습니다 — 걷는 동작이 빠졌다, 미소가 너무 크다, 물건이 문턱을 넘었다, 문틀이 휘었다.
  2. 원인일 수 있는 지시나 빠진 전환을 찾습니다. 가설로 다룹니다.
  3. 해당 지시를 고치고 모순되는 문구를 지웁니다.
  4. 다른 설정과 레퍼런스 역할은 가능한 한 그대로 둡니다.
  5. 새 결과에서 고쳐진 것과 의도치 않게 바뀐 것을 함께 봅니다.

프롬프트가 길다고 좋거나 나쁘지 않습니다. 이 프로젝트에는 긴 프롬프트에서 나온 채택본도 있습니다. 일관된 지시, 명확한 물리적 순서, 가능한 타이밍이 글자 수보다 중요합니다.

10. 제외한 것과 한계

채택된 프롬프트에 있었다는 이유만으로 낡은 씬 설정을 재사용하지 않습니다. 이전 프롬프트에는 방문자의 음성 대답, “breathing sway”, 옛 문 방향이 들어 있었고, 이것들은 현재 제작 규칙과 충돌해 고정 블록에서 뺐습니다.

이전 프롬프트에 40mm 지시가 있었지만 왜곡 문제의 검증된 해법이 아닙니다. 재사용 블록은 특정 초점거리를 강제하지 않고 프레이밍과 직선 유지 지시만 남겼습니다.

보존 문구는 모델에 대한 요청이지 픽셀 고정이 아닙니다. 끝 구간이 반드시 그대로여야 하면, 그 원본 푸티지를 최종 편집에 남기고 이음매를 확인하세요.

목적 있는 동작은 짧게 유지하되 움직임을 보여줄 시간은 줍니다. 15초를 불필요한 정지로 채우지도, 인물이 순간이동해 보일 만큼 걷기를 압축하지도 않습니다. 4초 상호작용 구간은 의도된 것이고, 그 밖의 정지는 씬에 기여해야 합니다.

1. Status and provenance

All videos identified as preferred results were generated with Seedance 2.5. The settings below come from the working conversation and supplied screenshots, not from independently recovered generation metadata for every preferred clip. Asset-selection guidance is a synthesis of the workflow.

Quoted instructions and reusable prompt blocks are excerpts assembled from previously used prompts. They are not a claim that every sentence was present in every preferred result.

Not validated

The yellow-paper scene is an unvalidated new direction, not an established successful example.

Earlier preferred scenes also used other durations, including 19 seconds. Fifteen seconds is the current working unit, not a universal requirement.

Two established workflows

  • Start-frame generation — establish the composition with a still, then animate from that visual state. The prompt behind the preferred low-distortion example said explicitly: “Continue directly from the supplied starting frame.”
  • Forward extension — continue from an accepted clip's actual ending. This produced the accepted entrance sequence, 03_1.mp4.

Neither workflow guarantees continuity. Inspect movement, dialogue, identity, and the join before accepting a clip. High bitrate reduces compression; it is not an acting or identity control.

3. Three traps found in the logs

Found by querying the generation history directly. None of these are in the original guide.

Trap 1 — the parameter wins on duration

Writing Create an 8-second… in the prompt while duration is set to 15 produces a 15-second clip. The reverse also happened: a prompt saying Create a 15-second… with the parameter at 11 produced 11 seconds. Both cases are in the log.

Every timestamp inside the prompt (0.0–1.0, 4.0–7.5) must be recomputed against the real length, or dialogue gets cut off and the tail runs empty.

There are three places to change. ①the duration parameter ②the “N-second” in the prompt's first line ③the beat timestamps. Always change all three.

Trap 2 — aspect ratio is meaningless in extension mode

Forward / Backward extensions and video-edit jobs show aspect_ratio as 16:9 in the log, yet the actual output is 480×854 (9:16). In extension and edit modes the source video's ratio wins and the parameter is ignored.

So a “16:9” reading in the parameters is not a problem. What actually matters is whether the source clip itself is 9:16. A landscape source yields a landscape result.

Trap 3 — conversation text leaking into the prompt field

The log contains three consecutive generations whose prompt body begins with a Korean chat sentence — an explanation written for a person, pasted into the prompt field by mistake.

The model reads it as instruction. Keep only English direction in the prompt field, and check that the first line starts with Create a… or Extend the… before submitting.

5. Fixed first-person prompt block

This block combines sentences actually used in earlier prompts. Add the scene's actions, exact dialogue, and timing separately. The share-house arrival description is specific to this project.

ONE uninterrupted take, entirely from the first-person viewpoint of a new resident arriving at a Japanese share house. The camera IS the visitor's eyes. All movement happens in real time within one continuous physical space.

CAMERA AND FRAMING
Strict eye-level first-person viewpoint.
Maintain a fixed focal length throughout.

Door frames, roof edges, wall corners, and tile lines remain straight across the image.
Natural perspective convergence during head tilts is acceptable, but architectural lines must not bow or curve.

Frame only what is naturally visible from the visitor's position.
Allow the roof, ground, suitcase, and Hinata's lower body to extend outside the frame.
Do not widen the lens to fit everything into one image.

When stopped, retain very slight natural postural sway.
Camera movement must not bend, stretch, or warp the environment.
Never show the visitor's face or full body.

Camera movement is visual only and must not produce audible breathing.

CHARACTER PERFORMANCE
Whenever she addresses the visitor, she looks directly into the camera lens.
Play this with small, believable reactions rather than broad comedy.
Preserve her normal facial proportions.
No exaggerated wide eyes, stretched mouth, or theatrical shock.

GLOBAL INTERACTIVE AUDIO RULE
The visitor NEVER produces audible dialogue, breathing, sighs, gasps, humming, laughter, or other vocal sounds.
Natural camera movement is visual only and must not be accompanied by breath noise.
Footsteps, suitcase wheels, and physical interaction sounds are allowed.

Whenever the script reaches a visitor response, replace the spoken response with approximately FOUR SECONDS of the other character looking into the lens and naturally waiting or listening.
No audible visitor answer, subtitles, response text, or countdown.

Use occasional natural blinking and tiny relaxed posture adjustments.
Her mouth remains relaxed and still.
She does not speak again or react as though an answer has already been confirmed.

CONTINUOUS ACTION
These timestamps are successive beats within ONE uninterrupted take, not separate shots.
Preserve all actions in order.
Use normal-speed movement with connected transitions between actions.

FINAL CONSTRAINTS
No cuts, hidden cuts, transitions, montage, reverse angles, inserts, time jumps, slow motion, or speed ramps.
No subtitles, captions, speech bubbles, countdowns, logos, or text overlays.
Scope warning

The relaxed-still-mouth and “no acknowledgment” instructions apply during the response window only, not during dialogue.

If the next scripted action is an acknowledgment, place it explicitly after those four seconds. The accepted entrance flow used a small nod and 「はい！あ、どうぞ。」 after the waiting interval.

7. Movement instructions

Add these only when needed.

Stationary conversation — from the preferred low-distortion example

Maintain the same eye-level conversational viewpoint as the starting frame.

No walking forward, zooming, deliberate nodding, or camera movement to follow Hinata's glances.

Keep Hinata's face and upper body readable within the vertical frame.

Walking and relocation — from the entrance prompts

Walking produces gentle footstep-induced bob and small irregular balance adjustments.

Hinata must visibly walk whenever her location changes.
Keep her in view throughout her movement from the doorway to the raised wooden floor. Show her crossing the tiled entrance and stepping onto the wood.
Her position changes through continuous walking, without skipped movement or sudden relocation.

Do not apply stationary-camera instructions to a walking segment. Replace the doorway-to-floor route with the actual route for later scenes.

From the log — a walking camera is a person, not a rig

If the camera glides, it reads as equipment. The sentences actually used:

Each footstep lands with a small whole-frame DIP of a few centimetres that rises back by
the next step - once per step, the rhythm of a real person, not a shake. The frame
SWAYS a little as the weight shifts, and the gaze LEADS: the eyes turn first, the camera
follows a beat later. NOT a dolly, NOT a gimbal glide, NOT a steadicam drift.
If the frame moves smoothly like camera equipment, the frame is wrong.

Note the last line's shape: If X, the frame is wrong. Stating the failure condition lands better than “don't do X.”

8. Keep these scene-specific

  • Exact spoken lines and their speakers. Exclude incorrect-answer branches from the default generation.
  • Where the four-second response window occurs and what expression is held during it.
  • Character starting and ending positions.
  • Visible walking routes and hand–object contact.
  • Door state, hinge location, permitted movement.
  • Prop ownership and where props remain afterward.
  • The final state needed for the next continuation.

Current project continuity

  • Visitor's left hand pulls the mint suitcase; right hand presses the exterior intercom.
  • Visitor sleeves are white; Hinata's sweater is pink.
  • The suitcase stays on the lower entrance tiles, toward the left, clear of the central path and door swing.
  • Shoes are removed offscreen when scripted. Only the visitor steps onto the wooden floor; the suitcase stays behind.
  • Preserve architecture already visible in the source. Do not invent another door to repair an inconsistent door state.

9. Directing natural acting

The methods below describe how the prompts behind the natural-feeling results were constructed. They are practical directing principles inferred from this project, not experimentally isolated causes of model behaviour. No wording guarantees a result.

① Give the actor a reason to behave, then specify the visible response

An emotional label alone leaves most of the performance undefined. Establish what the character expects, what changes, and what they try to hide. Then describe a few small, observable reactions.

Hinata expected the visitor at six o'clock.
She is surprised by the early arrival and is trying to remain polite while something unfinished inside the house distracts her.

This recognition interrupts her movement: she briefly stops, her upper eyelids lift slightly, and her lips part gently.

The pattern is expectation → trigger → small physical reaction → social recovery. Avoid piling several strong emotions onto one moment. Mild embarrassment reads through a hesitant gaze and a restrained smile — it does not need a gasp, a flinch, and a large backward movement all at once.

② Write transitions between expressions, not a list of poses

The smile develops across the movement rather than snapping on instantly.

The movements flow together without rigidly stopping in separate poses.

Her initial surprise softens into polite uncertainty.

Let a reaction have a cause: she notices the visitor before becoming surprised, and begins recovering her politeness before addressing them again. Do not require a smile to remain unchanged while the character processes new information.

③ Specify intensity through a few facial cues

The smile is modest and a little effortful, not a broad grin.

She is slightly flustered and self-conscious, not frightened, angry, or suspicious.

Keep her cheeks relaxed and her smile restrained.

The useful instruction is the visible performance itself. A long list of prohibited expressions is not a substitute. If a portrait shows a broad toothy smile but the scene needs a faint one, omit that portrait or give it a narrowly defined role.

④ Direct Japanese delivery as conversation, not timed recitation

Speak the exact Japanese sentence as one connected natural question.

Do not slow the speech to fill the time or insert long pauses between words.

The initial 「あ」 is a small realization. The rest is polite acknowledgment with a lightly questioning inflection.

Write the exact line and explain its conversational purpose: acknowledgment, cautious confirmation, a quiet aside, a quick attempt to change the subject. Allocate waiting time after the sentence rather than stretching syllables. When a line needs urgency, specify brisk conversational delivery instead of more volume.

⑤ Make gaze purposeful and listener reactions responsive

When Hinata addresses the visitor, she looks directly into the camera lens.
When she addresses the man inside, she turns toward the interior, then returns to the lens afterward.

Her eyes shift toward the living-room direction inside, followed by a small head turn.

The camera stays on Hinata; do not pan or cut to the suitcase or clock.
From the log — separate the eyes from the head

This structure drew the strongest “that looks human” response. Pin the eyes to move while the head explicitly does not follow.

HER EYES ALONE travel downward toward the floor in front of you.
HER HEAD DOES NOT FOLLOW - the chin stays put; only eyelids and pupils move.

⑥ Treat the response window as active listening

Occasional natural blinking, a slight softening around the eyes, and tiny relaxed posture adjustments keep her alive.

No freeze frame, slow motion, additional dialogue, silent mouthing, repeated nods, or exaggerated reactions.

Once the full four-second pause has ended, Hinata gives a small, natural nod as though she has heard the visitor's greeting.

Do not ask for frequent nods, repeated glances, and gestures merely to fill silence. A quiet smile and an occasional blink are enough. The visitor stays vocally silent throughout.

⑦ Give each physical action a beginning, contact, and end

“She waits inside” describes a destination; it does not explain how she got there.

Keeping one hand on the door, she extends her free hand toward the hallway with an open, gently upward-facing palm.

Hinata releases the front door, which remains open.
She turns partly toward the interior and visibly takes two or three small steps across the tiled entrance, then steps onto the raised wooden floor.

The visitor parks the suitcase upright beside their left leg, clear of the front door's swing and the path onto the wooden floor, and releases the handle.

Keep position changes visible when they matter. Do not hide the actor with a downward camera tilt at exactly the moment you ask them to relocate. Establish what stays behind: once the visitor releases the suitcase, it remains on the tiles instead of following the camera.

⑧ Overlap actions when the performance calls for it

Do not insert a pause between blocking the paper and beginning the line. The movement, head turn, and start of speech overlap naturally.

Speech can begin during a head turn or as a step finishes. This communicates urgency without exaggeration. This particular paper-scene direction has not been confirmed successful. Use overlap to express a clear intention, not to squeeze unrelated actions into an impossible time window.

⑨ Keep the camera physically motivated

The upward look and left-right glances are small physical head rotations.

Keep them compact and human, not sweeping cinematic pans.

Frame only what is naturally visible from the visitor's position.
Do not widen the lens to fit everything into one image.

A small head turn to investigate, a short forward move to enter, a modest rise to step onto a raised floor. Do not add camera movement merely because a scene is described as cinematic.

From the log — angle and eye height as numbers

The lens was specified as a diagonal field of view in degrees and an eye height in metres, not in mm.

LENS - 65 degree diagonal field of view, eye level 1.55 m, axis level unless the beat
says otherwise. NO zoom, NO push-in, NO pull-out, NO focus pull. Shot size comes from
DISTANCE only.

“Shot size comes from DISTANCE only” is the operative clause. Sizing a shot with zoom breaks the first-person illusion.

From the log — pin framing as a proportion

Instead of “medium shot,” the framing was pinned as a share of the frame.

HER SHOULDERS SPAN ABOUT FORTY PERCENT OF THE FRAME WIDTH and her head fills about
one seventh of the frame height, from the very first frame to the very last.
THE BOTTOM EDGE OF THE PICTURE CROSSES HER LEGS JUST BELOW THE KNEE.
If her whole body from head to toe comes into the picture, the shot is wrong.
From the log — the hand needs its own spec

In first person, any hand that enters frame must be the same hand every time. This is also where finger collapse gets blocked.

THE HAND IS THE SAME HAND IN EVERY SHOT: a young woman's right hand, slim
medium-length fingers, narrow wrist, small narrow back of the hand, tapered fingertips,
short bare nails, smooth skin, no standing tendons. No ring, polish, watch or bracelet.
FIVE SEPARATE FINGERS with clear shadow gaps - four fingers plus one thumb, correct
anatomy, no fused or melted fingers.

⑩ Preserve what worked and revise one problem at a time

When a clip is accepted, use its actual final state as the next starting condition. Keep the successful dialogue and staging intact when fixing a small issue. If the source door is closed, do not silently describe it as already open in the continuation.

  1. Identify the visible failure precisely: missing walking steps, an overly broad smile, an object crossing the threshold, a bowed door frame.
  2. Find the instruction or omitted transition that could be contributing. Treat it as a hypothesis.
  3. Change that direction and remove contradictory wording.
  4. Keep other settings and reference roles stable where practical.
  5. Inspect the new result for both the requested correction and unintended changes.

Long prompts are not automatically better or worse — this project produced preferred results from detailed prompts. Prioritise consistent instructions, clear physical sequences, and achievable timing over word count.

10. Exclusions and practical limits

Do not reuse outdated scene details simply because they appeared in a preferred prompt. Earlier prompts included an audible visitor reply, “breathing sway”, and an older door orientation. These conflict with current production rules and were excluded from the fixed block.

A 40mm instruction appeared in an earlier prompt, but it is not a verified solution to distortion. The reusable block keeps framing and straight-line instructions without making a focal length mandatory.

Preservation language is a request to the model, not a pixel lock. If an ending must remain exactly unchanged, retain that original footage in the final edit and inspect the join.

Keep purposeful actions brief, but allow enough time to show movement. Do not fill 15 seconds with unnecessary pauses, or compress walking until characters appear to teleport. The four-second interaction window is intentional; other pauses should serve the scene.
