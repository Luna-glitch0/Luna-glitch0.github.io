인사이트 요약

3분이면 읽습니다. 실제로 결과를 바꾼 것만 남겼습니다.

연기가 어색한 이유는 대개 «잘못 적어서» 가 아니라 «안 적어서» 입니다. 비워둔 자리는 모델이 채우고, 대개 과하게 채웁니다.

1

가장 자주 놓치는 여덟 가지

왼쪽은 실제로 프롬프트에 적는 영어 문구입니다. 이렇게만 적으면 가운데 같은 영상이 나옵니다.

이렇게만 적으면이런 영상이 나온다이렇게 고친다
she is surprised 놀라는 정도를 모델이 알아서 정합니다. 대개 필요 이상으로 크게 합니다 놀란 이유를 먼저 쓰고, 눈꺼풀·입술이 어떻게 움직이는지까지 적습니다
she smiles 첫 프레임부터 끝까지 같은 웃는 얼굴이 붙어 있어 표정이 굳어 보입니다 미소가 언제 시작해 언제 누그러지는지를 적습니다
she looks down 고개까지 같이 내려가서 얼굴이 화면 밖으로 나갑니다 HER HEAD DOES NOT FOLLOW 를 덧붙여 눈만 움직이게 합니다
she waits 표정이 멈춰버리거나, 반대로 고개를 계속 끄덕입니다 눈 깜빡임과 아주 작은 자세 변화를 직접 지정합니다
she walks forward 사람이 걷는 게 아니라 카메라가 레일 위를 미끄러지듯 움직입니다 걸음마다 화면이 한 번 툭 내려갔다 올라오게 적습니다
she waits inside 걷는 장면 없이 위치만 바뀝니다 어디를 거쳐 몇 걸음으로 가는지 적습니다
show the whole entrance 다 담으려고 화각이 넓어져서 문틀과 벽선이 휩니다 지붕·바닥·짐은 화면 밖으로 나가도 된다고 적습니다
Do not … 스무 줄 «그럼 어느 정도로 하라는 건지» 기준이 없어 그대로입니다 원하는 그림을 먼저 쓰고, If X, the shot is wrong 한 줄을 답니다
시간은 파라미터가 이깁니다.

프롬프트 첫 줄의 «15초» 를 고쳐도 duration 파라미터가 그대로면 소용없습니다. 길이를 바꿀 때는 파라미터 · 첫 줄 · 비트 타임스탬프 세 곳을 같이 고칩니다.

2

자연스러움을 만드는 다섯

효과가 가장 컸던 것들입니다. 그대로 붙여 쓸 수 있습니다.

① 시선과 고개를 나눈다

«사람 같다» 는 반응을 가장 많이 얻은 문장입니다. 눈만 움직이고 고개는 따라가지 않는다고 못 박습니다.

HER EYES ALONE travel downward toward the floor in front of you.
HER HEAD DOES NOT FOLLOW - the chin stays put; only eyelids and pupils move.

② 카메라를 장비가 아니라 사람으로 만든다

1인칭에서 프레임이 매끄럽게 미끄러지면 즉시 «촬영 장비» 로 읽힙니다. 걸음마다 한 번씩 내려갔다 올라와야 합니다.

Each footstep lands with a small whole-frame DIP of a few centimetres that rises
back by the next step - once per step, the rhythm of a real person, not a shake.
The gaze LEADS: the eyes turn first, the camera follows a beat later.
If the frame moves smoothly like camera equipment, the frame is wrong.

③ 침묵을 연기시킨다

유저가 대답할 5초는 빈 화면이 아니라 «듣고 있는 연기» 입니다. 그리고 배우는 아직 답을 못 들은 상태여야 합니다.

Occasional natural blinking, a slight softening around the eyes, and tiny relaxed
posture adjustments keep her alive.
No freeze frame, repeated nods, silent mouthing, or exaggerated reactions.
Once the full five-second pause has ended, Hinata gives a small, natural nod.

④ 부정은 «그러면 틀린 것이다» 로 쓴다

«하지 마라» 는 피할 것만 알려줍니다. 실패 조건은 판정 기준을 주기 때문에 모델이 스스로 확인합니다.

If the frame moves smoothly like camera equipment, the frame is wrong.
If her whole body from head to toe comes into the picture, the shot is wrong.

⑤ 렌즈와 손을 숫자로 못 박는다

«미디엄 숏» 은 프레임마다 다르게 해석돼 인물 크기가 슬금슬금 변합니다. 그리고 1인칭에서 손은 유일한 신체 신호라 컷마다 달라지면 바로 티가 납니다.

LENS - 65 degree diagonal field of view, eye level 1.55 m.
NO zoom, NO push-in. Shot size comes from DISTANCE only.

THE HAND IS THE SAME HAND IN EVERY SHOT: a young woman's right hand, slim
medium-length fingers, short bare nails. No ring, polish, watch or bracelet.
FIVE SEPARATE FINGERS with clear shadow gaps, no fused or melted fingers.
3

레퍼런스 한 가지만 기억한다면

사진이 지시문을 이깁니다.

레퍼런스가 활짝 웃고 있으면 «옅게 웃어라» 라고 써도 활짝 웃습니다. 씬에 안 맞는 사진은 설명을 붙일 게 아니라 빼야 합니다.

그리고 레퍼런스를 넣을 때마다 그 사진이 무엇을 정의하는지 한 줄을 같이 씁니다. 역할을 안 주면 서로 다투다가, 히나타의 분홍 스웨터가 1인칭 방문자에게 옮겨갑니다.

5

1인칭 공통 프롬프트

어떤 1인칭 영상이든 앞에 그대로 붙일 수 있는 부분입니다. 프로젝트 설정은 빼두었습니다.

이 블록을 먼저 붙이고, 그 아래에 씬의 동작 · 대사 · 타이밍만 적습니다.

[ ] 로 감싼 세 곳만 바꿔 쓰시면 됩니다 — 누구의 시점인지, 눈높이, 화면에 들어오는 손.

ONE uninterrupted take, entirely from the first-person viewpoint of [a visitor arriving at
a house]. The camera IS their eyes. All movement happens in real time within one continuous
physical space.

CAMERA
Strict eye-level first-person viewpoint. Maintain a fixed focal length throughout.
LENS - 65 degree diagonal field of view, eye level [1.55] m, axis level unless a beat says
otherwise. NO zoom, NO push-in, NO pull-out, NO focus pull.
Shot size comes from DISTANCE only.

Door frames, roof edges, wall corners and tile lines remain straight across the image.
Natural perspective convergence during head tilts is acceptable, but architectural lines
must not bow or curve.
Frame only what is naturally visible from this position. Allow the ceiling, the ground and
anything being carried to extend outside the frame.
Do not widen the lens to fit everything into one image.

MOVEMENT
When standing still, retain a very slight natural postural sway.
When walking, each footstep lands with a small whole-frame DIP of a few centimetres that
rises back by the next step - once per step, the rhythm of a real person, not a shake.
The frame SWAYS a little as the weight shifts, and the gaze LEADS: the eyes turn first,
the camera follows a beat later.
NOT a dolly, NOT a gimbal glide, NOT a steadicam drift.
If the frame moves smoothly like camera equipment, the frame is wrong.
Camera movement must not bend, stretch or warp the environment.

THE VIEWER'S BODY
Never show the viewer's face, head, shoulders or torso. No mirror, no reflection of the
viewer, no camera-operator shadow.
Any hand that enters frame is THE SAME HAND IN EVERY SHOT: [a young woman's right hand,
slim medium-length fingers, narrow wrist, tapered fingertips, short bare nails, smooth
skin]. No ring, polish, watch or bracelet.
FIVE SEPARATE FINGERS with clear shadow gaps - four fingers plus one thumb, correct
anatomy, no fused or melted fingers.

THE OTHER PERSON
Whenever they address the viewer, they look directly into the camera lens.
When they address someone else, they turn toward that person, then return to the lens
afterward.
Play reactions small and believable rather than broad. Preserve normal facial proportions.
No exaggerated wide eyes, stretched mouth, or theatrical shock.

AUDIO
The viewer NEVER produces audible dialogue, breathing, sighs, gasps, humming, laughter or
other vocal sounds. Camera movement is visual only and must not be accompanied by breath
noise. Footsteps and physical interaction sounds are allowed.

WHEN THE VIEWER WOULD SPEAK
Replace the viewer's reply with approximately FIVE SECONDS of the other person looking into
the lens and naturally waiting or listening. No audible reply, no subtitles, no response
text, no countdown.
During those seconds use occasional natural blinking, a slight softening around the eyes,
and tiny relaxed posture adjustments. Their mouth remains relaxed and still. They do not
speak again or react as though an answer has already been confirmed.
Any acknowledgment happens ONLY AFTER the full five seconds have elapsed.

CONTINUOUS ACTION
The timestamps below are successive beats within ONE uninterrupted take, not separate shots.
Preserve all actions in order, at normal speed, with connected transitions between them.

NEVER
No cuts, hidden cuts, transitions, montage, reverse angles, inserts, time jumps, slow motion
or speed ramps.
No subtitles, captions, speech bubbles, countdowns, logos or text overlays.
이 블록에 씬 내용을 섞지 않습니다.

동작 · 대사 · 타이밍을 여기 끼워 넣으면, 다음 씬에서 어디를 고쳐야 하는지 알 수 없게 됩니다. 공통은 위에, 씬별은 아래에 두 덩어리로 유지합니다.

Key insights

A three-minute read. Only what actually changed the results.

Acting usually goes wrong not because you wrote it badly, but because you never wrote it at all. The model fills in whatever you leave blank — and it almost always overdoes it.

1

The eight things people miss most

The left column is what you actually type into the prompt. Write only that, and the middle column is what comes back.

Write only thisAnd you get thisFix it like this
she is surprised The model decides how big the surprise is — usually bigger than you wanted Write why she is surprised, then how the eyelids and lips move
she smiles The same smile sits on her face from the first frame to the last, so it reads as frozen Write when it begins and when it softens
she looks down The head goes down with the eyes and the face leaves the frame Add HER HEAD DOES NOT FOLLOW so only the eyes move
she waits Her face stops dead, or she nods over and over instead Specify the blinking and the tiny postural shifts
she walks forward The camera glides along a rail instead of a person walking Make the frame dip once per footstep and rise back
she waits inside Her position changes with no walking in between Write the route and how many steps it takes
show the whole entrance The lens widens to fit it all, and door frames and wall lines bow Say the roof, floor and luggage may leave the frame
twenty lines of Do not … It never answers “then how much?”, so nothing actually changes Describe the shot you want first, then add one If X, the shot is wrong
The parameter wins on duration.

Changing “15-second” in the first line does nothing while the duration parameter stays put. When you change length, change all three: the parameter, the first line, and the beat timestamps.

2

Five that make it look natural

These made the biggest difference. Paste them as they are.

① Split the eyes from the head

The line that drew the most “that looks human” responses. The eyes move and the head explicitly does not follow.

HER EYES ALONE travel downward toward the floor in front of you.
HER HEAD DOES NOT FOLLOW - the chin stays put; only eyelids and pupils move.

② Make the camera a person, not a rig

In first person, a smooth glide reads as camera equipment instantly. The frame must drop and rise once per step.

Each footstep lands with a small whole-frame DIP of a few centimetres that rises
back by the next step - once per step, the rhythm of a real person, not a shake.
The gaze LEADS: the eyes turn first, the camera follows a beat later.
If the frame moves smoothly like camera equipment, the frame is wrong.

③ Make the silence a performance

The five seconds where the viewer would be answering are not empty frames — they are the performance of listening. And the actor must not have heard an answer yet.

Occasional natural blinking, a slight softening around the eyes, and tiny relaxed
posture adjustments keep her alive.
No freeze frame, repeated nods, silent mouthing, or exaggerated reactions.
Once the full five-second pause has ended, Hinata gives a small, natural nod.

④ Write the failure condition, not the prohibition

“Do not” only names what to avoid. A failure condition supplies a test, so the model can check itself.

If the frame moves smoothly like camera equipment, the frame is wrong.
If her whole body from head to toe comes into the picture, the shot is wrong.

⑤ Pin the lens and the hand with numbers

“Medium shot” gets read differently from shot to shot, so the character drifts in size. And in first person the hand is your only body signal — if it changes between shots, everyone notices.

LENS - 65 degree diagonal field of view, eye level 1.55 m.
NO zoom, NO push-in. Shot size comes from DISTANCE only.

THE HAND IS THE SAME HAND IN EVERY SHOT: a young woman's right hand, slim
medium-length fingers, short bare nails. No ring, polish, watch or bracelet.
FIVE SEPARATE FINGERS with clear shadow gaps, no fused or melted fingers.
3

If you remember one thing about references

The image beats the sentence.

If the reference grins broadly, writing “smile faintly” will not stop it from grinning. A photo that does not fit the scene should be removed, not explained away.

And every time you add a reference, add one line saying what that image defines. Without assigned roles they fight each other, and Hinata's pink sweater ends up on the first-person visitor.
