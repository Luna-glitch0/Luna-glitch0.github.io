Part Ⅰ

시작하기

설정을 맞추고, 워크플로를 고르고, 고정 블록을 붙입니다.

워크플로 둘

스틸에서 시작
Start-frame 생성

구도를 스틸로 먼저 확정하고 그 상태에서 움직입니다. 왜곡이 가장 적었던 방식입니다.

이어 붙이기
Forward 확장

채택한 클립의 실제 마지막 프레임에서 잇습니다. 입장 씬이 이 방식으로 나왔습니다.

어느 쪽도 연속성을 보장하지 않습니다. 움직임·대사·인물 동일성·이음매를 눈으로 확인하고 채택합니다.

시작 프레임을 확실히 물리는 문장

@Image 1 IS THE FIRST FRAME. The shot begins exactly on it - same framing,
same objects in the same places, same light. Nothing is re-staged or added at the start.
Start image 프롬프트 — 실제 발췌 
Create a 15-second vertical 9:16 photorealistic Japanese live-action drama with synchronized Japanese dialogue and realistic location sound.

Continue directly from the supplied starting frame.

Match the starting frame's camera position, framing, Hinata's facial identity, hairstyle, clothing, door opening angle, hand placement, lighting, and entrance layout.
Extend Video 프롬프트 — 실제 발췌 
Extend the uploaded video FORWARD with a 15-second continuation in vertical 9:16.

Begin directly from the source video's actual final frame and continue its ongoing movement naturally. Preserve Hinata's position, pose, gaze direction, door angle, camera position, framing, lighting, and visible environment.

Maintain one uninterrupted first-person shot from the visitor's eye level. Do not restart the scene, replay preceding actions, or repeat dialogue from the source video.

All timestamps below refer only to the newly added 15-second continuation.

Forward 확장에서는 소스 영상만 올리고 Start / End 이미지는 넣지 않습니다. End 레퍼런스를 넣어도 끝 구간이 원본과 똑같이 남는다는 보장은 없습니다.

붙여넣는 고정 블록

씬이 바뀌어도 그대로 쓰는 부분입니다. 동작·대사·타이밍은 따로 붙입니다.

고정 1인칭 블록 전문 — 클릭해서 복사 
ONE uninterrupted take, entirely from the first-person viewpoint of a new resident arriving at a Japanese share house. The camera IS the visitor's eyes. All movement happens in real time within one continuous physical space.

CAMERA AND FRAMING
Strict eye-level first-person viewpoint.
Maintain a fixed focal length throughout.

Door frames, roof edges, wall corners, and tile lines remain straight across the image.
Natural perspective convergence during head tilts is acceptable, but architectural lines must not bow or curve.

Frame only what is naturally visible from the visitor's position.
Allow the roof, ground, suitcase, and Hinata's lower body to extend outside the frame.
Do not widen the lens to fit everything into one image.

When stopped, retain very slight natural postural sway.
Camera movement must not bend, stretch, or warp the environment.
Never show the visitor's face or full body.

Camera movement is visual only and must not produce audible breathing.

CHARACTER PERFORMANCE
Whenever she addresses the visitor, she looks directly into the camera lens.
Play this with small, believable reactions rather than broad comedy.
Preserve her normal facial proportions.
No exaggerated wide eyes, stretched mouth, or theatrical shock.

GLOBAL INTERACTIVE AUDIO RULE
The visitor NEVER produces audible dialogue, breathing, sighs, gasps, humming, laughter, or other vocal sounds.
Natural camera movement is visual only and must not be accompanied by breath noise.
Footsteps, suitcase wheels, and physical interaction sounds are allowed.

Whenever the script reaches a visitor response, replace the spoken response with approximately FOUR SECONDS of the other character looking into the lens and naturally waiting or listening.
No audible visitor answer, subtitles, response text, or countdown.

Use occasional natural blinking and tiny relaxed posture adjustments.
Her mouth remains relaxed and still.
She does not speak again or react as though an answer has already been confirmed.

CONTINUOUS ACTION
These timestamps are successive beats within ONE uninterrupted take, not separate shots.
Preserve all actions in order.
Use normal-speed movement with connected transitions between actions.

FINAL CONSTRAINTS
No cuts, hidden cuts, transitions, montage, reverse angles, inserts, time jumps, slow motion, or speed ramps.
No subtitles, captions, speech bubbles, countdowns, logos, or text overlays.
적용 범위를 착각하기 쉽습니다. «입을 편히 다물고 있다», «대답을 들은 것처럼 굴지 않는다» 는 4초 응답 구간에만 적용됩니다. 대사 중에는 아닙니다. 다음 동작이 «알아들었다» 는 반응이라면 4초 뒤에 명시적으로 배치합니다. 채택된 입장 씬은 그 뒤에 작은 끄덕임과 「はい！あ、どうぞ。」 를 넣었습니다.
Part Ⅱ

연기를 쓰는 법

감정 이름을 적는 것으로는 연기가 정해지지 않습니다.

① 이유를 주고, 보이는 반응을 지정한다

감정 이름 대신 «무엇을 예상했고, 무엇이 달라졌고, 무엇을 감추려 하는지» 를 씁니다.

패턴은 기대 → 계기 → 작은 신체 반응 → 사회적 수습 입니다. 한 순간에 강한 감정을 여러 개 쌓지 않습니다. 가벼운 민망함은 머뭇거리는 시선과 절제된 미소로 충분하고, 숨 들이키기·움찔·크게 물러서기를 동시에 시킬 필요가 없습니다.

실제 발췌 
Hinata expected the visitor at six o'clock.
She is surprised by the early arrival and is trying to remain polite while something unfinished inside the house distracts her.

This recognition interrupts her movement: she briefly stops, her upper eyelids lift slightly, and her lips part gently.

② 포즈 목록이 아니라 «전환» 을 쓴다

미소가 언제 시작하고 누그러지고 사라지는지를 씁니다. 하나의 표정을 씬 내내 유지시키지 않습니다.

반응에는 원인이 있어야 합니다. 방문자를 먼저 알아보고 놀라고, 다시 말을 걸기 전에 예의를 회복하기 시작합니다. 새 정보를 처리하는 동안 미소가 그대로일 것을 요구하지 않습니다.

실제 발췌 
The smile develops across the movement rather than snapping on instantly.

The movements flow together without rigidly stopping in separate poses.

Her initial surprise softens into polite uncertainty.

③ 세기는 얼굴 단서 몇 개로 정한다

쓸모 있는 지시는 «보이는 연기 그 자체» 입니다. 금지 표정을 길게 나열하는 것으로 대신할 수 없습니다.

편안한 볼, 살짝 올라간 입꼬리, 눈썹 안쪽의 작은 들림처럼 구체적인 단서를 씁니다. 레퍼런스 사진이 활짝 웃는데 씬은 옅은 미소가 필요하다면, 그 사진을 빼거나 역할을 좁게 한정합니다.

실제 발췌 
The smile is modest and a little effortful, not a broad grin.

She is slightly flustered and self-conscious, not frightened, angry, or suspicious.

Keep her cheeks relaxed and her smile restrained.

④ 대사는 낭독이 아니라 대화다

정확한 대사를 적고 «그 말의 대화적 목적» 을 함께 설명합니다.

알아들었다는 표시인지, 조심스러운 확인인지, 혼잣말인지, 화제를 돌리려는 시도인지를 밝힙니다. 남는 시간은 문장 뒤에 배치하고, 음절을 늘여 채우지 않습니다. 급한 대사는 «크게» 가 아니라 «빠른 대화 속도» 로 지정합니다.

실제 발췌 
Speak the exact Japanese sentence as one connected natural question.

Do not slow the speech to fill the time or insert long pauses between words.

The initial 「あ」 is a small realization. The rest is polite acknowledgment with a lightly questioning inflection.
Part Ⅲ

시선 · 침묵 · 동작

1인칭에서 렌즈는 상대의 «눈» 입니다. 그래서 시선과 침묵이 연기의 절반입니다.

⑤ 시선과 고개를 분리한다

눈만 움직이고 고개는 따라가지 않게 못 박습니다. 가장 «사람 같다» 는 평을 받은 구조입니다.

HER EYES ALONE travel downward toward the floor in front of you.
HER HEAD DOES NOT FOLLOW - the chin stays put; only eyelids and pupils move.

시선을 돌릴 때는 구체적인 대상을 향하게 하고 자연스럽게 돌아오게 합니다. 카메라가 모든 시선을 따라갈 필요는 없습니다. 이렇게 하면 계속 응시하지 않아도 눈맞춤에 의미가 생깁니다.

실제 발췌 
When Hinata addresses the visitor, she looks directly into the camera lens.
When she addresses the man inside, she turns toward the interior, then returns to the lens afterward.

Her eyes shift toward the living-room direction inside, followed by a small head turn.

The camera stays on Hinata; do not pan or cut to the suitcase or clock.

⑥ 침묵도 연기다

4초 응답 구간은 정지 화면이 아니라 «적극적 듣기» 입니다.

침묵을 메우려고 잦은 끄덕임·반복적인 시선 이동·손짓을 요구하지 않습니다. 조용한 미소와 이따금의 눈 깜빡임으로 충분합니다. 그리고 배우는 아직 답을 못 들은 상태입니다. «알아들었다» 는 반응은 4초가 끝난 뒤에 옵니다.

실제 발췌 
Occasional natural blinking, a slight softening around the eyes, and tiny relaxed posture adjustments keep her alive.

No freeze frame, slow motion, additional dialogue, silent mouthing, repeated nods, or exaggerated reactions.

Once the full four-second pause has ended, Hinata gives a small, natural nod as though she has heard the visitor's greeting.

⑦ 동작에 시작 · 접촉 · 끝을 준다

«그녀가 안에서 기다린다» 는 도착지를 말할 뿐, 어떻게 갔는지를 설명하지 않습니다.

경로와, 몸·손·물건의 관계를 씁니다. 중요한 위치 변화는 보이게 둡니다 — 인물이 이동하는 바로 그 순간에 카메라를 아래로 틸트해 가리지 않습니다. 그리고 무엇이 남는지를 정합니다. 방문자가 손잡이를 놓으면 캐리어는 타일 위에 남지, 카메라를 따라오지 않습니다.

실제 발췌 
Keeping one hand on the door, she extends her free hand toward the hallway with an open, gently upward-facing palm.

Hinata releases the front door, which remains open.
She turns partly toward the interior and visibly takes two or three small steps across the tiled entrance, then steps onto the raised wooden floor.

The visitor parks the suitcase upright beside their left leg, clear of the front door's swing and the path onto the wooden floor, and releases the handle.

필요하면 동작을 겹칩니다. 말은 고개를 돌리는 도중이나 발을 딛는 끝에서 시작할 수 있고, 이게 과장 없이 급박함을 만듭니다. 다만 무관한 동작 여러 개를 불가능한 시간에 밀어넣는 데 쓰지 않습니다.

겹치기 — 실제 발췌 
Do not insert a pause between blocking the paper and beginning the line. The movement, head turn, and start of speech overlap naturally.
Part Ⅳ

카메라와 1인칭 규격

1인칭이 깨지는 자리는 대개 카메라와 손입니다.

⑧ 카메라는 장비가 아니라 사람이다

프레임이 매끄럽게 미끄러지면 그 순간 «장비» 로 읽힙니다. 걸음마다 한 번씩 내려갔다 올라와야 합니다.

Each footstep lands with a small whole-frame DIP of a few centimetres that rises back by
the next step - once per step, the rhythm of a real person, not a shake. The frame
SWAYS a little as the weight shifts, and the gaze LEADS: the eyes turn first, the camera
follows a beat later. NOT a dolly, NOT a gimbal glide, NOT a steadicam drift.
If the frame moves smoothly like camera equipment, the frame is wrong.

카메라 움직임에는 물리적 이유가 있어야 합니다. 무언가를 살피려는 작은 고개 돌림, 들어가려는 짧은 전진, 마루에 오르는 완만한 상승. «시네마틱» 이라는 이유만으로 카메라를 움직이지 않습니다.

고정 카메라 지시를 걷는 구간에 그대로 쓰지 않습니다. 대화 씬용 «움직이지 마라» 와 이동 씬용 «걸음마다 흔들려라» 는 서로 충돌합니다.
고정 대화 · 걷기 — 실제 발췌 
[ 고정 대화 ]
Maintain the same eye-level conversational viewpoint as the starting frame.
No walking forward, zooming, deliberate nodding, or camera movement to follow Hinata's glances.
Keep Hinata's face and upper body readable within the vertical frame.

[ 걷기 · 이동 ]
Walking produces gentle footstep-induced bob and small irregular balance adjustments.
Hinata must visibly walk whenever her location changes.
Keep her in view throughout her movement from the doorway to the raised wooden floor. Show her crossing the tiled entrance and stepping onto the wood.
Her position changes through continuous walking, without skipped movement or sudden relocation.

⑨ 렌즈와 프레이밍은 숫자로 못 박는다

렌즈는 mm 가 아니라 «대각 화각(도)» 과 «눈높이(m)» 로 지정합니다. 숏 크기는 오직 거리로 정합니다.

LENS - 65 degree diagonal field of view, eye level 1.55 m, axis level unless the beat
says otherwise. NO zoom, NO push-in, NO pull-out, NO focus pull. Shot size comes from
DISTANCE only.

줌으로 크기를 만들면 1인칭이 깨집니다. 프레이밍도 «미디엄 숏» 같은 말 대신 화면에서 차지하는 비율로 못 박습니다.

HER SHOULDERS SPAN ABOUT FORTY PERCENT OF THE FRAME WIDTH and her head fills about
one seventh of the frame height, from the very first frame to the very last.
THE BOTTOM EDGE OF THE PICTURE CROSSES HER LEGS JUST BELOW THE KNEE.
If her whole body from head to toe comes into the picture, the shot is wrong.
한 화면에 전부 담으려 하지 않습니다. 천장·인물 전신·바닥·손·짐을 모두 넣으라고 요구하면 렌즈가 넓어지고 건축선이 휩니다. 지붕·바닥·캐리어·하반신은 프레임 밖으로 나가도 됩니다.

⑩ 손은 매번 같은 손이어야 한다

화면에 들어오는 손에 따로 규격을 줍니다. 손가락 붕괴도 여기서 막습니다.

THE HAND IS THE SAME HAND IN EVERY SHOT: a young woman's right hand, slim
medium-length fingers, narrow wrist, small narrow back of the hand, tapered fingertips,
short bare nails, smooth skin, no standing tendons. No ring, polish, watch or bracelet.
FIVE SEPARATE FINGERS with clear shadow gaps - four fingers plus one thumb, correct
anatomy, no fused or melted fingers.

1인칭에서는 얼굴과 몸이 절대 보이지 않아야 하므로, 손이 유일한 신체 신호입니다. 그래서 손만은 인물 레퍼런스만큼 상세히 적습니다.

Part Ⅴ

프롬프트를 쓰는 법

같은 내용을 어떻게 적는지가 결과를 바꿉니다.

⑪ 부정은 «그러면 틀린 것이다» 로 쓴다

«하지 마라» 보다 «그렇게 되면 틀린 그림이다» 가 더 잘 듣습니다.

If the frame moves smoothly like camera equipment, the frame is wrong.
If her whole body from head to toe comes into the picture, the shot is wrong.

금지 목록을 길게 늘어놓는 대신 실패 조건을 한 줄로 못 박습니다. 그리고 원하는 그림을 먼저 적습니다 — 금지문은 그것을 보조할 때만 의미가 있습니다.

⑫ 된 것은 지키고, 문제는 하나씩 고친다

클립을 채택하면 그 «실제 마지막 상태» 를 다음 시작 조건으로 씁니다.

작은 문제를 고칠 때 성공한 대사와 동선은 그대로 둡니다. 소스에서 문이 닫혀 있으면, 연결본에서 열려 있다고 슬쩍 바꿔 쓰지 않습니다.

  1. 보이는 실패를 정확히 짚습니다 — 걷는 동작이 빠졌다, 미소가 너무 크다, 물건이 문턱을 넘었다, 문틀이 휘었다.
  2. 원인일 수 있는 지시나 빠진 전환을 찾습니다. 가설로 다룹니다.
  3. 해당 지시를 고치고 모순되는 문구를 지웁니다.
  4. 다른 설정과 레퍼런스 역할은 가능한 한 그대로 둡니다.
  5. 새 결과에서 고쳐진 것과 의도치 않게 바뀐 것을 함께 봅니다.

프롬프트가 길다고 좋거나 나쁘지 않습니다. 일관된 지시, 명확한 물리적 순서, 가능한 타이밍이 글자 수보다 중요합니다.

Part Ⅵ

씬마다 정하는 것

고정 블록에 넣지 않고, 새 씬마다 따로 씁니다.

  • 정확한 대사와 화자. 오답 분기는 기본 생성에서 뺍니다.
  • 4초 응답 구간이 어디이고, 그동안 어떤 표정을 유지하는지

이 프로젝트의 연속성 규칙

  • 방문자의 왼손은 민트색 캐리어를 끌고, 오른손으로 외부 인터폰을 누릅니다.
  • 방문자 소매는 흰색, 히나타 스웨터는 분홍색.
  • 캐리어는 현관 타일 바닥 왼쪽에 둡니다. 가운데 통로와 문이 열리는 궤적을 비웁니다.
  • 신발은 대본에 있을 때 화면 밖에서 벗습니다. 마루에 올라가는 것은 방문자뿐이고 캐리어는 남습니다.
  • 소스에 이미 보이는 건축을 유지합니다. 문 상태가 안 맞는다고 없던 문을 새로 만들지 않습니다.

하지 말 것과 한계

«채택된 프롬프트에 있었다» 는 이유만으로 재사용하지 않습니다.

고정 블록에서 뺀 것

  • 방문자의 음성 대답 — 지금은 4초 침묵으로 대체합니다.
  • “breathing sway” — 카메라 움직임에 숨소리가 붙습니다.
  • 옛 문 방향 — 현재 건축과 충돌합니다.

검증되지 않은 것

  • 40mm 지시는 왜곡 문제의 해법이 아닙니다. 재사용 블록은 특정 초점거리를 강제하지 않고 프레이밍과 직선 유지 지시만 남겼습니다.
  • 노란 종이 씬은 아직 성공이 확인되지 않은 새 방향입니다.

원리적 한계

보존 문구는 모델에 대한 요청이지 픽셀 고정이 아닙니다. 끝 구간이 반드시 그대로여야 하면, 그 원본 푸티지를 최종 편집에 남기고 이음매를 확인하세요.

목적 있는 동작은 짧게 유지하되 움직임을 보여줄 시간은 줍니다. 15초를 불필요한 정지로 채우지도, 인물이 순간이동해 보일 만큼 걷기를 압축하지도 않습니다. 4초 상호작용 구간은 의도된 것이고, 그 밖의 정지는 씬에 기여해야 합니다.

The fixed block

This part stays the same across scenes. Attach actions, dialogue, and timing separately.

Full fixed first-person block — click to copy 
ONE uninterrupted take, entirely from the first-person viewpoint of a new resident arriving at a Japanese share house. The camera IS the visitor's eyes. All movement happens in real time within one continuous physical space.

CAMERA AND FRAMING
Strict eye-level first-person viewpoint.
Maintain a fixed focal length throughout.

Door frames, roof edges, wall corners, and tile lines remain straight across the image.
Natural perspective convergence during head tilts is acceptable, but architectural lines must not bow or curve.

Frame only what is naturally visible from the visitor's position.
Allow the roof, ground, suitcase, and Hinata's lower body to extend outside the frame.
Do not widen the lens to fit everything into one image.

When stopped, retain very slight natural postural sway.
Camera movement must not bend, stretch, or warp the environment.
Never show the visitor's face or full body.

Camera movement is visual only and must not produce audible breathing.

CHARACTER PERFORMANCE
Whenever she addresses the visitor, she looks directly into the camera lens.
Play this with small, believable reactions rather than broad comedy.
Preserve her normal facial proportions.
No exaggerated wide eyes, stretched mouth, or theatrical shock.

GLOBAL INTERACTIVE AUDIO RULE
The visitor NEVER produces audible dialogue, breathing, sighs, gasps, humming, laughter, or other vocal sounds.
Natural camera movement is visual only and must not be accompanied by breath noise.
Footsteps, suitcase wheels, and physical interaction sounds are allowed.

Whenever the script reaches a visitor response, replace the spoken response with approximately FOUR SECONDS of the other character looking into the lens and naturally waiting or listening.
No audible visitor answer, subtitles, response text, or countdown.

Use occasional natural blinking and tiny relaxed posture adjustments.
Her mouth remains relaxed and still.
She does not speak again or react as though an answer has already been confirmed.

CONTINUOUS ACTION
These timestamps are successive beats within ONE uninterrupted take, not separate shots.
Preserve all actions in order.
Use normal-speed movement with connected transitions between actions.

FINAL CONSTRAINTS
No cuts, hidden cuts, transitions, montage, reverse angles, inserts, time jumps, slow motion, or speed ramps.
No subtitles, captions, speech bubbles, countdowns, logos, or text overlays.
The scope is easy to get wrong. The relaxed-still-mouth and “no acknowledgment” lines apply during the response window only, not during dialogue. If the next action is an acknowledgment, place it explicitly after those four seconds. The accepted entrance flow used a small nod and 「はい！あ、どうぞ。」 afterwards.
Part Ⅱ

Writing performance

Naming an emotion does not define the performance.

① Give a reason, then specify the visible response

Instead of an emotion label, write what the character expected, what changed, and what they are trying to hide.

The pattern is expectation → trigger → small physical reaction → social recovery. Do not pile several strong emotions onto one moment. Mild embarrassment reads through a hesitant gaze and a restrained smile; it does not need a gasp, a flinch, and a large backward movement at once.

Actual excerpt 
Hinata expected the visitor at six o'clock.
She is surprised by the early arrival and is trying to remain polite while something unfinished inside the house distracts her.

This recognition interrupts her movement: she briefly stops, her upper eyelids lift slightly, and her lips part gently.

② Write transitions, not a list of poses

Write when the smile begins, softens, and fades. Do not make one expression hold for the whole scene.

Let a reaction have a cause: she notices the visitor before becoming surprised, and begins recovering her politeness before addressing them again. Do not require a smile to stay unchanged while the character processes new information.

Actual excerpt 
The smile develops across the movement rather than snapping on instantly.

The movements flow together without rigidly stopping in separate poses.

Her initial surprise softens into polite uncertainty.

③ Set intensity with a few facial cues

The useful instruction is the visible performance itself. A long list of prohibited expressions is not a substitute.

Use concrete cues — relaxed cheeks, slightly raised mouth corners, a small lift of the inner eyebrows. If a portrait shows a broad toothy smile but the scene needs a faint one, omit that portrait or give it a narrowly defined role.

Actual excerpt 
The smile is modest and a little effortful, not a broad grin.

She is slightly flustered and self-conscious, not frightened, angry, or suspicious.

Keep her cheeks relaxed and her smile restrained.

④ Dialogue is conversation, not recitation

Write the exact line and explain its conversational purpose.

Say whether it is acknowledgment, cautious confirmation, a quiet aside, or an attempt to change the subject. Allocate waiting time after the sentence rather than stretching syllables. When a line needs urgency, specify brisk conversational delivery instead of more volume.

Actual excerpt 
Speak the exact Japanese sentence as one connected natural question.

Do not slow the speech to fill the time or insert long pauses between words.

The initial 「あ」 is a small realization. The rest is polite acknowledgment with a lightly questioning inflection.
Part Ⅲ

Gaze · silence · action

In first person the lens is the other person's eyes. Gaze and silence are half the performance.

⑤ Separate the eyes from the head

Pin the eyes to move while the head explicitly does not follow. This drew the strongest “that looks human” response.

HER EYES ALONE travel downward toward the floor in front of you.
HER HEAD DOES NOT FOLLOW - the chin stays put; only eyelids and pupils move.

When the gaze leaves, send it toward a specific object or event, then bring it back naturally. The camera does not need to follow every glance. This makes eye contact meaningful without constant staring.

Actual excerpt 
When Hinata addresses the visitor, she looks directly into the camera lens.
When she addresses the man inside, she turns toward the interior, then returns to the lens afterward.

Her eyes shift toward the living-room direction inside, followed by a small head turn.

The camera stays on Hinata; do not pan or cut to the suitcase or clock.

⑥ Silence is performance too

The four-second response window is active listening, not a frozen frame.

Do not ask for frequent nods, repeated glances, or gestures merely to fill the silence. A quiet smile and an occasional blink are enough. And the actor has not heard an answer yet — acknowledgment comes only after the interval.

Actual excerpt 
Occasional natural blinking, a slight softening around the eyes, and tiny relaxed posture adjustments keep her alive.

No freeze frame, slow motion, additional dialogue, silent mouthing, repeated nods, or exaggerated reactions.

Once the full four-second pause has ended, Hinata gives a small, natural nod as though she has heard the visitor's greeting.

⑦ Give each action a start, contact, and end

“She waits inside” names a destination; it does not explain how she got there.

Write the route and the relationship between body, hands, and objects. Keep meaningful position changes visible — do not hide the actor with a downward camera tilt at exactly the moment you ask them to relocate. And establish what stays behind: once the visitor releases the handle, the suitcase remains on the tiles instead of following the camera.

Actual excerpt 
Keeping one hand on the door, she extends her free hand toward the hallway with an open, gently upward-facing palm.

Hinata releases the front door, which remains open.
She turns partly toward the interior and visibly takes two or three small steps across the tiled entrance, then steps onto the raised wooden floor.

The visitor parks the suitcase upright beside their left leg, clear of the front door's swing and the path onto the wooden floor, and releases the handle.

Overlap actions when the performance calls for it. Speech can begin during a head turn or as a step finishes, which communicates urgency without exaggeration. Do not use overlap to squeeze unrelated actions into an impossible time window.

Overlap — actual excerpt 
Do not insert a pause between blocking the paper and beginning the line. The movement, head turn, and start of speech overlap naturally.
Part Ⅳ

Camera and first-person spec

First person usually breaks at the camera and the hands.

⑧ The camera is a person, not a rig

The moment the frame glides smoothly, it reads as equipment. It must dip once per footstep and rise back.

Each footstep lands with a small whole-frame DIP of a few centimetres that rises back by
the next step - once per step, the rhythm of a real person, not a shake. The frame
SWAYS a little as the weight shifts, and the gaze LEADS: the eyes turn first, the camera
follows a beat later. NOT a dolly, NOT a gimbal glide, NOT a steadicam drift.
If the frame moves smoothly like camera equipment, the frame is wrong.

Camera movement needs a physical motivation: a small head turn to investigate, a short move forward to enter, a modest rise to step onto a raised floor. Do not move the camera merely because a scene is described as cinematic.

Do not apply stationary-camera instructions to a walking segment. “Do not move” for a conversation and “bob with every step” for a relocation contradict each other.
Stationary · walking — actual excerpts 
[ Stationary conversation ]
Maintain the same eye-level conversational viewpoint as the starting frame.
No walking forward, zooming, deliberate nodding, or camera movement to follow Hinata's glances.
Keep Hinata's face and upper body readable within the vertical frame.

[ Walking and relocation ]
Walking produces gentle footstep-induced bob and small irregular balance adjustments.
Hinata must visibly walk whenever her location changes.
Keep her in view throughout her movement from the doorway to the raised wooden floor. Show her crossing the tiled entrance and stepping onto the wood.
Her position changes through continuous walking, without skipped movement or sudden relocation.

⑨ Pin lens and framing with numbers

Specify the lens as a diagonal field of view in degrees and an eye height in metres — not in mm. Shot size comes from distance only.

LENS - 65 degree diagonal field of view, eye level 1.55 m, axis level unless the beat
says otherwise. NO zoom, NO push-in, NO pull-out, NO focus pull. Shot size comes from
DISTANCE only.

Sizing a shot with zoom breaks the first-person illusion. Pin framing as a share of the frame rather than saying “medium shot”.

HER SHOULDERS SPAN ABOUT FORTY PERCENT OF THE FRAME WIDTH and her head fills about
one seventh of the frame height, from the very first frame to the very last.
THE BOTTOM EDGE OF THE PICTURE CROSSES HER LEGS JUST BELOW THE KNEE.
If her whole body from head to toe comes into the picture, the shot is wrong.
Do not try to fit everything into one frame. Asking for the ceiling, the full actor, the floor, the hands, and the luggage all at once widens the lens and bows the architecture. The roof, ground, suitcase, and lower body are allowed to leave the frame.

⑩ The hand must be the same hand every time

Give any hand that enters frame its own spec. This is also where finger collapse gets blocked.

THE HAND IS THE SAME HAND IN EVERY SHOT: a young woman's right hand, slim
medium-length fingers, narrow wrist, small narrow back of the hand, tapered fingertips,
short bare nails, smooth skin, no standing tendons. No ring, polish, watch or bracelet.
FIVE SEPARATE FINGERS with clear shadow gaps - four fingers plus one thumb, correct
anatomy, no fused or melted fingers.

Since the face and body must never appear, the hand is the only body signal you have. So describe it as carefully as a character reference.

Part Ⅴ

How to write the prompt

How you phrase the same content changes the result.

⑪ State the failure condition, not the prohibition

“If it looks like X, the shot is wrong” lands better than “do not do X”.

If the frame moves smoothly like camera equipment, the frame is wrong.
If her whole body from head to toe comes into the picture, the shot is wrong.

Instead of a long list of prohibitions, pin the failure condition in one line. And describe the wanted image first — a prohibition only helps when it supports that.

⑫ Preserve what worked; fix one thing at a time

When a clip is accepted, use its actual final state as the next starting condition.

Keep the successful dialogue and staging intact when fixing a small issue. If the source door is closed, do not silently describe it as already open in the continuation.

  1. Identify the visible failure precisely: missing walking steps, an overly broad smile, an object crossing the threshold, a bowed door frame.
  2. Find the instruction or omitted transition that could be contributing. Treat it as a hypothesis.
  3. Change that direction and remove contradictory wording.
  4. Keep other settings and reference roles stable where practical.
  5. Inspect the new result for both the correction and unintended changes.

Long prompts are not automatically better or worse. Prioritise consistent instructions, clear physical sequences, and achievable timing over word count.

Part Ⅵ

Decide per scene

Keep these out of the fixed block and write them fresh for each segment.

  • Exact spoken lines and their speakers. Exclude incorrect-answer branches from the default generation.
  • Where the four-second response window occurs and what expression is held during it.
  • Character starting and ending positions.
  • Visible walking routes and hand–object contact.
  • Door state, hinge location, permitted movement.
  • Prop ownership and where props remain afterward.
  • The final state needed for the next continuation.

Current project continuity

  • Visitor's left hand pulls the mint suitcase; right hand presses the exterior intercom.
  • Visitor sleeves are white; Hinata's sweater is pink.
  • The suitcase stays on the lower entrance tiles, toward the left, clear of the central path and door swing.
  • Shoes are removed offscreen when scripted. Only the visitor steps onto the wooden floor; the suitcase stays behind.
  • Preserve architecture already visible in the source. Do not invent another door to repair an inconsistent door state.

Part Ⅷ

Exclusions and limits

Do not reuse something merely because it appeared in a preferred prompt.

Removed from the fixed block

  • An audible visitor reply — now replaced by the four-second silence.
  • “breathing sway” — it attaches breath noise to camera movement.
  • The older door orientation — it conflicts with the current architecture.

Not validated

  • The 40mm instruction is not a verified solution to distortion. The reusable block keeps framing and straight-line instructions without mandating a focal length.
  • The yellow-paper scene is an unvalidated new direction.

Inherent limits

Preservation language is a request to the model, not a pixel lock. If an ending must remain exactly unchanged, retain that original footage in the final edit and inspect the join.

Keep purposeful actions brief, but allow enough time to show movement. Do not fill 15 seconds with unnecessary pauses, or compress walking until characters appear to teleport. The four-second interaction window is intentional; other pauses should serve the scene.
